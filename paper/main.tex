\section*{Abstract}
{\bf
How can a spectral function $A(\omega)$ from sample-based quantum diagonalization be tested without trusting it? Not with its own moments, which agree with it by construction, but with full-operator moments ${\langle J^\dagger(H-e_0)^kJ\rangle}$ of the probe $J$ on the subspace ground state, of energy $e_0$. We prove that the two differ exactly by the probe state's leak out of the subspace: the missed weight at $k=0$, and zero through order $2K+1$ when the subspace holds the probe state's order-$K$ Krylov space. Off an eigenstate the operator form matters: a lone commutator-form $m_1$ check can pass a first moment $11\%$ low. The $m_1$ and $m_2$ checks detect weight moved in frequency at fixed total weight, which captured weight cannot see. Scored against a numerical tolerance, this deterministic leak flags almost every sampled support and does not track the error, so detection and false-positive rates use exact moments plus shot noise. In a pre-registered blinded simulation, the \mbox{$m_0$--$m_2$} test rejected all $56$ truncations, $20$ of $34$ weight-preserving spurious features and none of $71$ under-converged Krylov spectra. Its false-positive rate was $1/112$, or $40/112$ under the pre-registered $1.96$-fold larger noise. No \mbox{$m_0$--$m_2$} check is guaranteed to detect weight moved among four or more fixed poles, and full-support Rayleigh--Ritz passes the default thresholds with relative $L_1$ error $0.93$. On IBM Heron samples, with all moments computed classically, captured weight rewards support spreading, not fidelity.
}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
Sample-based quantum diagonalization (SQD) reconstructs dynamical spectral functions $A(\omega)$ in sampled determinant subspaces~\cite{SQDspec2608,Patel2026}. This paper asks how such a reconstruction can be tested without being trusted. Moments read off $A(\omega)$ cannot test it, because they agree with it by construction. Moments of the full operators, evaluated on the subspace ground state from which $A(\omega)$ was built, can reject it. The probe state is the probe operator $J$ applied to that ground state. Order by order, the full-operator moments differ from the reconstruction's moments by exactly the probe state's leak out of the subspace (Proposition~\ref{prop:leak}).

Dynamical response functions such as the spectral function $A(k,\omega)$, the structure factors $S(q,\omega)$ and $S^{zz}(q,\omega)$ and the optical conductivity are measured by photoemission~\cite{Damascelli2003}, inelastic neutron scattering~\cite{Lake2005}, resonant inelastic x-ray scattering~\cite{Ament2011} and infrared spectroscopy~\cite{Basov2011}. Classically they are computed by many-body methods~\cite{Benthien2004,Qin2022}. Quantum algorithms for chemistry and materials began with ground-state energies~\cite{AspuruGuzik2005,Peruzzo2014,McArdle2020,Bauer2020}, and dynamical responses are now also computed on quantum processors.

Trapped ions have produced structure factors and spectral functions~\cite{GranetDSF2026,GranetARPES2026} and Green's functions built from measured Hamiltonian moments~\cite{GreeneDiniz2024}. Superconducting processors have produced an impurity Green's function for density-functional plus dynamical mean-field theory (DFT+DMFT) with up to 14 qubits~\cite{Selisko2025} and Green's functions of a four-site Hubbard model by Liouvillian recursion~\cite{Leblanc2026}. They have also produced the spin excitation spectrum of the one-dimensional Hubbard model on up to $30$ qubits~\cite{VilchezEstevez2025} and structure factors with up to 50 qubits, compared quantitatively with neutron data~\cite{Lee2026}.

SQD, quantum-selected configuration interaction (QSCI) and quantum Krylov methods were developed for ground states~\cite{QSCI2023,SQDchem2025,SKQD2501,Yoshioka2025,Motta2020}. They have been extended to excited states~\cite{Barison2024}, quasiparticle band structures~\cite{Ohgoe2025} and spectra~\cite{SQDspec2608,Patel2026}. The device samples a subspace; classical resources diagonalize the Hamiltonian in it and assemble $A(\omega)$. This is a quantum-centric supercomputing workflow~\cite{SQDchem2025,Alexeev2024}.

A spectrum reconstructed this way can be wrong in a way that a total-weight check misses: spectral weight sits at the wrong frequencies while the total weight and other integrated quantities stay within tolerance. We call this error weight misassignment; it is the error we target.

Critical studies document the ground-state limitations of QSCI and subspace diagonalization~\cite{Reinholdt2025,GaberleJattana2026,VaqueroSabater2026}, and error analyses bound how noise and ill-conditioning enter subspace and Krylov ground-state energies~\cite{Epperly2022,Kirby2024}. For sample-based spectral functions, our companion study shows this misassignment directly. There, on a subspace that holds the whole probe state, so that no weight is missed, the relative $L_1$ error is still $0.43$ ($L=6$ Hubbard ring, broadening $\eta=0.18\,t$ with $t$ the hopping)~\cite{SQDspec2608}. The same concern motivates response-targeted determinant selection on the classical side~\cite{LRSCI2025}.

Such errors matter most where no trusted classical cross-check exists. The edge of that regime moves as classical methods improve and demonstrations are dequantized~\cite{Preskill2018,Daley2022,Kim2023,Tindall2024,Tang2019,Hangleiter2023}; for sample-based diagonalization see Ref.~\cite{BonillaReviewSQD2026}. A check intended for that regime must first be calibrated where the ground truth is known~\mbox{\cite{Eisert2020,Carrasco2021,Elben2020,Lubinski2023,Flammia2011}}, as we do here.

Physics supplies an independent reference: every $T=0$ Lehmann spectrum obeys moment sum rules~\cite{HohenbergMartin1965,Kohn1964,Maldague1977} and positivity~\cite{Lehmann1954}, however it was computed. For a zero-temperature Lehmann function of a probe $J$, the power moments $m_k=\int\omega^kA(\omega)\,d\omega$ are ground-state expectation values, $m_k=\langle 0|J^\dagger(H-E_0)^kJ|0\rangle$. For a Hermitian probe $m_0=\langle J^2\rangle$ and $m_1=\tfrac12\langle[J,[H,J]]\rangle$~\cite{Lehmann1954,Kubo1957,HohenbergMartin1965}.

The test compares two sets of moments (Fig.~\ref{fig:hero}). We estimate the moments as $\widehat m_k=\langle O_k\rangle$ on a ground-state estimate, with $O_k\equiv J^\dagger(H-e_0)^kJ$ and $e_0$ the energy of that state, never by integrating $A(\omega)$. The reconstruction's own moments are $\bar m_k$, and the test statistic is $\Delta_k=|\widehat m_k-\bar m_k|$. A moment read back off $A(\omega)$ gives $\Delta_k\equiv0$; the operator estimate can disagree. The test battery, the checks for $k=0,1,2$, rejects the reconstruction when any one of them rejects. These are necessary conditions: failing one rejects the reconstruction, and passing does not show that it is correct.

\emph{Which estimator carries which result.} Section~\ref{sec:estimator} defines three estimators; two carry the results here, and the third, a bond-basis device estimator, is future work. The realizable estimator is the shared-state one, so called because it uses the reconstruction's own subspace ground state $|0_\mathcal S\rangle$: $\widehat m_k=\langle 0_\mathcal S|O_k|0_\mathcal S\rangle$. It is classical, needs only the sampled support and is deterministic. Its residual measures whether the reconstruction is closed under the full operators, not how far a moment is from the truth. Read against a numerical tolerance, it flags almost every sampled support, and its size does not track the reconstruction error (Sec.~\ref{sec:rr}). Detection and false-positive rates therefore come from exact moments or from the oracle estimator: the exact moment plus unbiased Gaussian shot noise, judged against thresholds $\tau_k$ that budget an assumed bias (Sec.~\ref{sec:estimator}). We name the estimator with each result.

\emph{(1) The leak hierarchy} (Proposition~\ref{prop:leak}, Sec.~\ref{sec:leak}). On the subspace ground state, the difference $\widehat m_k-\bar m_k$ is exactly the leak of the probe state $J|0_\mathcal S\rangle$ out of the subspace at order $k$. It is the missed spectral weight at $k=0$. When no weight is missed, the $k=2$ leak is the squared boundary coupling: the squared norm of the part of $HJ|0_\mathcal S\rangle$ outside the subspace. It vanishes through order $2K+1$ when the subspace contains the order-$K$ Krylov space of the probe state. The test needs only sparse matrix--vector products from $|0_\mathcal S\rangle$, not the exact ground state.

\emph{(2) The operator form matters off an eigenstate} (Sec.~\ref{sec:leak}). On a truncated subspace of a doped $L=6$ ring, $\langle O_1\rangle=4.558$, but the textbook double-commutator form gives $3.717$. At the default threshold $\tau_1=0.229$, a first-moment check in the $O_1$ form rejects a reconstruction whose first moment is $11\%$ low, and one in the double-commutator form passes it. The full battery rejects this truncation on $m_0$ in either form ($\Delta_0=12\,\tau_0$). The form therefore decides the verdict of checks that test $m_1$ without $m_0$. Examples are checks on spectra normalized to the $m_0$ sum rule~\cite{RoggeroCarlson2019} and on $A(k,\omega)$, whose $m_0$ is pinned to $1$. The same warning applies to any sum-rule check evaluated on a variational, subspace or truncated state.

\emph{(3) Misplaced weight at fixed total} (Secs.~\ref{sec:detectsim} and~\ref{sec:blind}). Under determinant truncation the operative channel is the zeroth-order leak, the probe weight that falls outside the sampled subspace; relative to $m_0$ it is the companion's captured-weight deficit. When weight moves in frequency at fixed total, $m_0$ is blind, and $m_1$ and $m_2$ reject the error. With exact moments and the default threshold rule, they reject it in the current response of the doped $L=12$ ring from a moved fraction $f\approx0.057$ and $0.046$, respectively. On that ring, the optical \textit{f}-sum moment $m_{-1}$ shifts most in relative terms, yet it rejects last. There the Drude stiffness cancels $94\%$ of the kinetic term that sets $m_{-1}$, so its threshold is wide. On ring and open chain alike, $m_{-1}$ rejects first only when the spurious weight lies at $0.5\,t$ or below.

In a pre-registered blinded simulation on the current probe of a doped $L=6$ ring, with the oracle estimator, the battery rejected all $56$ truncations. It rejected $25$ of $27$ weight-changing and $20$ of $34$ weight-preserving spurious features, and none of $71$ under-converged Krylov reconstructions. The $56$ Krylov reconstructions with two or more Lanczos nodes reproduce $m_0$--$m_2$ exactly; the $15$ one-node ones miss $m_2$ by $2.4$--$4.2\%$, within $\tau_2$. The false-positive rate was $1/112$ with the simulated noise the threshold assumes. With the $1.96$-fold larger noise the pre-registration specified, it was $40/112$, and noise also rejected $23$ of the Krylov reconstructions, $17$ of them exact through $m_2$.

\emph{(4) Limits of low-order sum-rule checks} (Sec.~\ref{sec:pass}). With $m_0$--$m_2$, the guaranteed power against fixed-pole weight redistributions is zero for every spectrum with four or more distinct poles. On the five-pole $L=6$ current spectrum, a redistribution that moves $72\%$ of the weight passes. At this order the classical Chebyshev--Markov--Stieltjes bracket on the cumulative weight of a passing reconstruction is vacuous at the spectral mean. The zero guaranteed power and the vacuous bracket hold for every sum-rule validation at this order, not only for this test. On the illustrative $U/t=8$ current measure of the doped $L=6$ ring, the default tolerances loosen the exact-moment bound on the weight a passing reconstruction can place above the band: $3.2$--$7.7$-fold just above it and about tenfold far above it.

Consider a redistribution of fixed size inside a pole cluster of diameter $D$ that preserves $m_0,\dots,m_{s-2}$. The shot cost of exposing it at order $s-1$ grows at least as $D^{-2(s-1)}$. With a bias floor, no shot budget exposes it at that order below a finite cluster size, although a higher tested order still can.

Sample-based reconstruction produces misplaced weight of its own that passes the default thresholds $\tau_k$. Rayleigh--Ritz on the full ground-state support of the calibration ring meets them, yet its relative $L_1$ error is $0.93$. Its only residual, the $k=2$ leak, is $1.9\%$ of $m_2$, so any tolerance on $m_2$ above that passes it; the default $\tau_2$ is $5.5\%$. Read against the numerical tolerance, its leak score flags it as not closed, but nothing in that score shows the error.

\emph{(5) Coverage is not fidelity, on device data} (Sec.~\ref{sec:device}). We evaluated supports sampled on the IBM Heron processor \texttt{ibm\_fez} for the electron-addition sector of an $L=8$ Hubbard ring. The device supplies only the supports: no moment is estimated from device counts, and every moment in this paper is evaluated classically. Here the probe state is built from the exact ground state, and the coverage residual $\Delta_0$ is its weight outside the sampled support. The residual grows from $0.0044$ to $0.37$ as coverage falls from $94\%$ to $35\%$. It rewards support spreading, not fidelity: at the largest shot budget, noise lowers it. At $5\times10^4$ shots per circuit the noisy device leaves less weight uncovered than all $2000$ noiseless replicas of its own circuits. A uniform in-sector sampler leaves less weight uncovered than the device at every budget. The smallest residual, $\Delta_0=0.0044$, coexists with a relative $L_1$ error of $0.35$.

\emph{Relation to the companion study.} The companion study reconstructs these spectra from sampled subspaces and bounds their error at exact-diagonalization scale, given the exact ground state~\cite{SQDspec2608}. We adopt its terms; its $\mu_j$ is our $m_j$. Several ingredients of this paper appear there first. Its captured weight $w$ gives our coverage residual $\Delta_0=m_0(1-w)$, the $k=0$ case of the leak with the probe state built from the exact ground state. Its second-moment deficit $\mu_2-\mu_2^{\mathcal S}=\lVert Q_{\mathcal S}HP_{\mathcal S}\varphi\rVert^2$, with $P_\mathcal S$ and $Q_\mathcal S$ the projectors onto the subspace and its complement, is the $k=2$ leak on a subspace of unit captured weight. The companion also states the classical moment exactness of Krylov projections~\cite{GM2010} and measures its reach. It compares reconstruction moments with exact ones on random coordinate subspaces, which already miss $m_0$, and observes that exact moments through order $2K+1$ coexist with an $O(1)$ reconstruction error. Finally, it shows that captured weight alone cannot certify a reconstruction; our $L=8$ device run is an instance of that result.

Relative to the companion study, this paper adds the full leak hierarchy on the subspace state as a thresholded rejection test for any diagonal $T=0$ Lehmann $A(\omega)$: single-particle, current or collective, and not only Rayleigh--Ritz reconstructions (items 1 and 2). It also adds what the test detects and its blinded calibration (item 3), the exact limits of a pass (item 4) and the device evaluation of the coverage channel against reference samplers (item 5).

\emph{Related work.} Sum-rule checks test spectra against Hamiltonian-derived moments. They are long-standing in dynamical mean-field theory and quantum Monte Carlo, most systematically in the sum-rule program of Turkowski and Freericks~\cite{TurkowskiFreericks2006,Freericks2009}. Analytic-continuation pipelines use sum rules as prior information~\cite{Jarrell1996} or compare with exact results~\cite{Sandvik1998}. For quantum-computed spectra, Buchs \emph{et al.}\ validate against experimental and classical anchors with metrics that include low-order moments and sum-rule residuals; they leave the regime without full classical benchmarks to future work~\cite{Buchs2026}. Moments also enter as inputs. In a quantum linear-response algorithm the zeroth moment restores the normalization of the computed response~\cite{RoggeroCarlson2019}. Frequency moments of spectral densities are obtained exactly from imaginary-time quantum Monte Carlo data~\cite{Dornheim2023moments}.

The classical moment problem, with its orthogonal polynomials and Gauss quadrature~\cite{ShohatTamarkin1943,Akhiezer1965,KreinNudelman1977,Simon1998,GM2010,Szego1975,GolubWelsch1969,Nevai1986,Lanczos1950}, underlies the kernel-polynomial and recursion methods~\cite{KPM2006,Haydock1972,Silver1996,Holzner2011}. It gave error bounds on spectroscopic line shapes from their equilibrium moments~\cite{Gordon1968} and Stieltjes--Tchebycheff photoabsorption profiles from dipole spectral moments~\cite{Langhoff1976}. It serves as an a posteriori bound on Lanczos quadrature~\cite{ChenTrogdonUbaru2021}, and it now bounds smeared spectral functions from Euclidean lattice correlators~\cite{AbbottJayOare2025,CausalBootstrap2026,CertifiedSpectral2026}. Semidefinite programs bound ground-state observables~\cite{WangAcin2024,Mortimer2026}.

Variational algorithms~\cite{Peruzzo2014,Cerezo2021,Bharti2022}, error mitigation~\cite{TemmeBravyi2017,Kandala2019,EndoBenjaminLi2018,vandenBerg2023,Cai2023} and randomized measurements~\cite{HuangKuengPreskill2020,Elben2023} act on states or expectation values, not on a reconstructed function. So do verification methods, from self-verifying variational simulation and state certification to observable estimation without classical verification~\cite{Kokail2019,HuangPreskillSoleimanifar2024,OEACV2026,Kraft2026}. Testing a spectrum against sum rules is established practice; Table~\ref{tab:contribution} states what each approach compares against.

\section{Moment tests, their estimators and the leak identity}
\label{sec:method}
\label{sec:role}
The test applies to the output of a sample-based-diagonalization pipeline; its residual on the subspace state is an exact operator leak (Proposition~\ref{prop:leak}).

\emph{Notation.} The hopping $t$ is the energy unit throughout; a frequency rescaled to $[-1,1]$ is written $x$ (Appendix~\ref{app:proof}). $J$ always denotes the probe operator, and the spin-exchange constant $4t^2/U$ is written $J_{\mathrm{ex}}$. $K$ is the battery order, the highest moment order the test uses; in Theorem~\ref{thm:detect} it is the highest order a redistribution preserves, and in the moment-exactness statement of Proposition~\ref{prop:leak} the order of a Krylov space.

\subsection{The pipeline and the origin of the samples}
In sample-based diagonalization~\cite{SQDchem2025,QSCI2023,SKQD2501} the processor does not evaluate $A(\omega)$: each computational-basis shot of a prepared state returns a bitstring (a candidate Slater determinant). In the simplest form, used here and in the companion, the distinct bitstrings surviving post-selection to the physical particle-number and spin sector form the subspace $\mathcal{S}$~\cite{QSCI2023}; SQD instead repairs the bitstrings by configuration recovery and builds $\mathcal S$ from products of the sampled spin-up and spin-down strings~\cite{SQDchem2025}. The Hamiltonian is projected onto $\mathcal{S}$ and diagonalized classically, and the spectrum is the $T=0$ Lehmann sum
\begin{equation}
A(\omega)=\sum_n|\langle n|J|0\rangle|^2\,\delta_\eta\big(\omega-(E_n-E_0)\big)
\label{eq:lehmann}
\end{equation}
over the resulting eigenpairs, with $\delta_\eta$ a narrow broadening~\cite{Lehmann1954}. In the companion's formulation the prepared state is a Trotterized real-time evolution of the $(N{+}1)$-particle seed $c^{\dagger}_p|\psi_0\rangle$~\cite{SQDspec2608}: a Trotterized Krylov evolution as in Krylov quantum diagonalization~\cite{Yoshioka2025}, sampled as in sample-based Krylov diagonalization~\cite{SKQD2501} and related to time-evolved QSCI~\cite{Mikkelsen2024}. All circuits run on hardware here (the $L=6$ jobs of 2026-08-21 and the $L=8$ jobs of 2026-08-29) and in the companion are number- and $S_z$-conserving samplers of the addition sector, not the Hubbard propagator of the seed; for the companion's run this is stated in Ref.~\cite{SQDspec2608}. Neither the companion's run~\cite{SQDspec2608} nor ours used the configuration recovery of SQD~\cite{SQDchem2025}. The intended role of the processor appears only at scale, where the configurations carrying the weight of the probe state cannot in general be enumerated classically: an ideal sampler of the targeted state would concentrate shots on them and fix $\mathcal{S}$ by measurement rather than by a classical selection heuristic~\cite{SQDchem2025}. We do not show that any device sampler does this better than a classical heuristic, and the companion claims no quantum advantage~\cite{SQDspec2608}.

\subsection{Moments, three estimators and the rejection test}\label{sec:estimator}
Evaluated with the exact eigenpairs of $H$ and $\eta\to0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:lehmann} is the zero-temperature diagonal Lehmann spectral function of the probe $J$, a positive measure supported on ${\omega=E_n-E_0\ge0}$~\cite{Lehmann1954}. On an exact eigenstate $|0\rangle$ its power moments are ground-state expectation values (Appendix~\ref{app:moments})~\cite{HohenbergMartin1965},
\begin{equation}
m_k=\int\omega^kA(\omega)\,d\omega=\langle 0|J^\dagger(H-E_0)^kJ|0\rangle=\langle 0|J^\dagger\,\mathrm{ad}_H^{k}(J)|0\rangle ,
\label{eq:moment-identity}
\end{equation}
with $\mathrm{ad}_H(J)\equiv[H,J]$, so that ${m_0=\langle J^\dagger J\rangle}$ and, for a Hermitian probe, ${m_0=\langle J^2\rangle}$ and ${m_1=\tfrac{1}{2}\langle [J,[H,J]]\rangle}$. For the current probe $m_0$ is the total current--current weight and $m_1/m_0$ the mean frequency; the regular part of the optical \textit{f}-sum is set by the separate moment $m_{-1}$ (on a ring the Drude weight carries the rest; Appendix~\ref{app:moments})~\cite{Kohn1964,Maldague1977}. On a state $|\psi\rangle$ that need not be an eigenstate we define the moment operator and its commutator form,
\begin{equation}
O_k\equiv J^\dagger(H-e_0)^kJ,\qquad C_k\equiv J^\dagger\,\mathrm{ad}_H^{k}(J),\qquad e_0=\langle\psi|H|\psi\rangle .
\label{eq:Ok}
\end{equation}
On an exact eigenstate $e_0=E_0$ and $\langle O_k\rangle=\langle C_k\rangle=m_k$; off an eigenstate the two in general differ for $k\ge1$ and neither equals $m_k$. The estimator is $\widehat m_k=\langle\psi|O_k|\psi\rangle$; $C_k$ is used only on exact eigenstates (Sec.~\ref{sec:leak} gives the reason). The reconstruction moment $\bar{m}_k = \int \omega^k A(\omega)\,d\omega$ is the quantity a circular check would use, and the test statistic is $\Delta_k = |\widehat{m}_k - \bar{m}_k|$. We evaluate $\bar m_k$ from the poles and residues of the reconstruction, at $\eta\to0$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-recon}], never by integrating a broadened $A(\omega)$ on a grid: a Lorentzian has no finite first or second moment, and grid integration biases the moments by an amount that depends on the window. A circular check compares $\bar{m}_k$ with itself, returns $\Delta_k \equiv 0$ and can never reject. We use three estimators, defined once here.

\emph{(a) Shared-state estimator (realizable now, classical).} $\widehat m_k=\langle 0_\mathcal S|O_k|0_\mathcal S\rangle$ on the reconstruction's own subspace ground state, evaluated with the full sparse operators: with $\varphi_\mathcal S=J|0_\mathcal S\rangle$, $\widehat m_{2j}=\|(H-e_0)^j\varphi_\mathcal S\|^2$ and $\widehat m_{2j+1}=\langle(H-e_0)^j\varphi_\mathcal S|(H-e_0)|(H-e_0)^j\varphi_\mathcal S\rangle$, so the battery through $k=2$ needs $\varphi_\mathcal S$ on the configurations that $J$ connects to $\mathcal S$ and one further application of $H$. It needs only the sampled support. It is deterministic, and its residual is exactly the leak of Proposition~\ref{prop:leak}, so it measures whether the reconstruction is closed under the full operators, not how far a moment is from the truth. We report its residual as the leak score $\Delta_k/\widehat m_k$, judged against a numerical tolerance of $10^{-6}$ (on the $112$ faithful controls of the blinded test the score is below $3\times10^{-15}$). Through $k=2$ the three scores vanish exactly when $\operatorname{span}\mathcal S$ contains $\varphi_\mathcal S$ and $H\varphi_\mathcal S$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:leak}), so at this tolerance the test flags almost every sampled support; its informative output is the vector of leak scores, whose size does not track the reconstruction error (Sec.~\ref{sec:rr}). Applying $\tau_k$ below to it is an explicit convention calibrated on the oracle estimator; a pass in this paper is a pass under $\tau_k$, and we name the estimator each time.

\emph{(b) Oracle estimator (the calibration model: exact moments plus shot noise).} The exact moment $m_k$ plus unbiased Gaussian noise of variance $\operatorname{Var}(O_k)/N_s$, with $\operatorname{Var}(O_k)$ the local-estimator variance of Appendix~\ref{app:estimator}. It stands for an unbiased finite-shot estimate of the true moment, and the blinded calibration of Sec.~\ref{sec:blind} uses it. For it the reconstruction is rejected when $\Delta_k>\tau_k$, with $\tau_k=z\,\delta_k$, $z=1.96$ ($\alpha=0.05$ per moment), and $\delta_k^2=\operatorname{Var}(O_k)/N_s+b_k^2$ the shot variance plus a bias budget $b_k$. The \emph{default rule} uses $N_s=5\times10^4$ and $b_k=0.02\,|\widehat m_k|$; for the three-moment battery its nominal family-wise level is $1-0.95^3=14.3\%$. The bias budget is assumed, not measured: the level, and the attribution of a rejection to the reconstruction rather than to the estimator, presuppose that the estimator's bias lies within $b_k$.

\emph{(c) Bond-basis device estimator (future).} $\langle O_k\rangle$ measured in qubit-wise-commuting Pauli groups, with rotated bases for the hopping terms, on a separately prepared ground state. It is verified in simulation only; its cost and forecast noise bias are in Appendix~\ref{app:native}.

The battery applies the moment tests for $k = 0,1,\dots,K$ (default: $K=2$) and rejects the reconstruction when any of them rejects; a reconstruction that no moment test rejects passes. At $k=0$, where $O_0=C_0=J^\dagger J$, the residual is the weight the support misses,
\[
\Delta_0=\langle\varphi|(\openone-\Pi_\mathcal{S})|\varphi\rangle=\|\varphi\|^2(1-w),\qquad w=\|\Pi_\mathcal{S}\varphi\|^2/\|\varphi\|^2 ,
\]
with $\Pi_\mathcal S$ the projector onto the span of $\mathcal S$ and $w$ the companion's captured weight~\cite{SQDspec2608}: for the shared-state estimator $|\varphi\rangle=J|0_\mathcal{S}\rangle$ and $\|\varphi\|^2=\widehat m_0$; for the device coverage residual $|\varphi\rangle=J|0\rangle$ on the exact ground state, with the addition probe $J=c^{\dagger}_{0\uparrow}$, and $\|\varphi\|^2=m_0$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:device}). Separately, the moments of every positive measure satisfy Hankel positivity $H_n=[\widehat m_{i+j}]\succeq 0$ and, for a diagonal $T=0$ Lehmann measure supported on ${\omega\ge 0}$, the shifted Stieltjes condition $H_n^{(1)}=[\widehat m_{i+j+1}]\succeq 0$~\cite{ShohatTamarkin1943,Akhiezer1965,KreinNudelman1977}. For a truncated sequence these conditions are not sufficient: $(m_0,m_1,m_2)=(1,0,1)$ satisfies both, yet no measure on $\omega\ge0$ has these moments. A violation rejects the estimate---device, mitigation or estimator---independently of $A(\omega)$; it cannot reject a non-negative reconstruction, whose own moments satisfy these conditions by construction (Appendix~\ref{app:stack}). The battery can also include an exactly known operator identity, such as a conserved charge $[H,G]=0$ with a known physical eigenvalue, whose violation rejects the prepared state, as in symmetry verification~\cite{BonetMonroig2018,McArdleYuanBenjamin2019} and Gauss-law oracles~\cite{Stryker2019}; Appendix~\ref{app:gauss} illustrates this with a Gauss law evaluated exactly on the state vector~\cite{Gauss2020}. The construction transports across models and observables because it needs only (a) a positive spectral measure and (b) operators whose ground-state expectations can be estimated without the reconstruction~\cite{KPM2006,GM2010}.

\subsection{The leak identity}\label{sec:leak}
Let $H_\mathcal{S}=\Pi_\mathcal{S}H\Pi_\mathcal{S}$, $|0_\mathcal S\rangle$ its ground state, $e_0=\langle 0_\mathcal{S}|H|0_\mathcal{S}\rangle$, $J_\mathcal{S}=\Pi_\mathcal{S}J\Pi_\mathcal{S}$ and $\varphi_\mathcal S\equiv J|0_\mathcal S\rangle$. A reconstruction built on $|0_\mathcal{S}\rangle$ has the moments $\bar m_k={\langle 0_\mathcal{S}|J_\mathcal{S}^\dagger(H_\mathcal{S}-e_0)^kJ_\mathcal{S}|0_\mathcal{S}\rangle}$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-recon}], with the convention $(H_\mathcal S-e_0)^0\equiv[\Pi_\mathcal S(H-e_0)\Pi_\mathcal S]^0\equiv\Pi_\mathcal S$.

\begin{proposition}[Leak identity]
\label{prop:leak}
With the shared-state estimator $\widehat m_k=\langle 0_\mathcal S|O_k|0_\mathcal S\rangle$ and the common reference $e_0$,
\begin{equation}
\widehat{m}_k-\bar m_k=\langle\varphi_\mathcal{S}|(H-e_0)^k|\varphi_\mathcal{S}\rangle-\langle\varphi_\mathcal{S}|[\Pi_\mathcal{S}(H-e_0)\Pi_\mathcal{S}]^{k}|\varphi_\mathcal{S}\rangle
\label{eq:app-leak}
\end{equation}
for every $k\ge0$. In particular: (i) at $k=0$ it is the missed weight $\widehat m_0(1-w)\ge0$; (ii) if $\operatorname{span}\mathcal S$ contains the Krylov space $\operatorname{span}\{\varphi_\mathcal S,H\varphi_\mathcal S,\dots,H^K\varphi_\mathcal S\}$, it vanishes for every $k\le2K+1$; (iii) if $w=1$, it vanishes at $k=1$, and at $k=2$ it equals the squared boundary coupling $\|(\openone-\Pi_\mathcal{S})H\varphi_\mathcal{S}\|^2\ge0$.
\end{proposition}

\noindent\emph{Proof.} Appendix~\ref{app:estimator}. Statement (ii) is the classical moment exactness of Krylov projections~\cite{Lanczos1950,GM2010}, which the companion states for coordinate subspaces and whose failure at order $2K+2$ it measures~\cite{SQDspec2608}; the $k=0$ and $k=2$ forms are the companion's captured-weight and second-moment results, stated there for the probe on the exact ground state. Their proofs use only the projector $\Pi_\mathcal S$ and a common energy shift, so they hold unchanged for $\varphi_\mathcal S$ and $e_0$. What the proposition adds is the whole hierarchy on the subspace state, which a pipeline holds: every order needs only sparse matrix--vector products from $|0_\mathcal S\rangle$.

\emph{Number-changing probes.} For a probe that changes the particle number, such as $J=c^\dagger_p$ of the single-particle Green's function, the ground state comes from an $N$-particle support $\mathcal S_0$ and the reconstruction lives on an $(N{\pm}1)$-particle support $\mathcal S$, built from the eigenpairs of $\Pi_\mathcal SH\Pi_\mathcal S$ with weights $|\langle n_\mathcal S|J|0_{\mathcal S_0}\rangle|^2$. Equation~\eqref{eq:app-leak} and statements (i)--(iii) then hold verbatim with $\varphi_\mathcal S=J|0_{\mathcal S_0}\rangle$, $e_0=\langle0_{\mathcal S_0}|H|0_{\mathcal S_0}\rangle$ and $\Pi_\mathcal S$ the projector onto $\mathcal S$, because the proof uses only $\varphi_\mathcal S$, $\Pi_\mathcal S$ and $e_0$; for the removal branch take $J=c_p$ and $\omega\to-\omega$.

The proposition fixes the operator form. With $O_k$ the residual is exactly the leak; with $C_k$ it would also contain $\langle 0_\mathcal{S}|(C_k-O_k)|0_\mathcal{S}\rangle$, which at $k=1$ equals $-\langle 0_\mathcal S|J^\dagger J\,(H-e_0)|0_\mathcal S\rangle$ and does not vanish off an eigenstate (Appendix~\ref{app:moments}). The difference changes verdicts. Take the current probe of the doped $L=6$, $U/t=4$ ring and truncate its sector to the $d=100$ determinants of largest $|\psi_0|^2$ ($44\%$ of the $225$-dimensional sector, $54\%$ of the $186$-determinant ground-state support). The subspace ground state gives $\langle O_1\rangle=4.558$ but $\tfrac12\langle[J,[H,J]]\rangle=3.717$, against the reconstruction moment $\bar m_1=3.743$ and the exact $m_1=4.227$. At the default threshold $\tau_1=0.229$ the $O_1$ form rejects the truncated reconstruction and the commutator form does not; with the truth known here, the rejection is the correct verdict, since $\bar m_1$ is $11\%$ below the exact value. At the six other cuts of the same scan the two forms give the same verdict (tie order and the full scan in Appendix~\ref{app:moments}). The battery as a whole rejects the $d=100$ cut on $m_0$ in either form, $\Delta_0=0.34=12\,\tau_0$, because $O_0=C_0$; the form decides the verdict of a check that tests $m_1$ without $m_0$. A first-moment check in the textbook double-commutator form, evaluated on a variational, subspace or truncated state, can thus pass a reconstruction whose first moment is wrong.

\section{What the moments detect (exact simulation)}
\label{sec:detectsim}
\label{sec:teeth}
\label{sec:observables}
Under determinant truncation the zeroth-order leak responds first; for weight moved in frequency at fixed total, only $m_1$ and $m_2$ respond. All results in this section are exact simulations.

\subsection{Truncation: the zeroth-order leak is the operative channel}
Determinant-subspace truncation is the native failure mode of sample-based diagonalization: the sampled subspace $\mathcal S$ omits configurations that carry spectral weight, and the reconstruction is internally consistent with an incomplete basis~\cite{SQDchem2025,QSCI2023}. We truncate the determinant subspace of the doped $L=12$ Hubbard ring ($N_\uparrow=N_\downarrow=4$, $U/t=8$; full support $n_{\mathrm{sup}}=2.4\times10^5$) to its top $d$ configurations by $|\psi_0|^2$, the noiseless limit of QSCI's selection of the most frequently sampled configurations~\cite{QSCI2023}, and evaluate both branches on the same subspace state $|0_\mathcal{S}\rangle$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:teethshared}). The circular control, $\bar m_1$ against itself, is zero by definition; the data show whether, and at what depth, the independent residuals cross a threshold.

The blinded simulation of Sec.~\ref{sec:blind} agrees: in the shared-state rescoring every one of its $56$ truncations is rejected on $m_0$, where the two operator forms coincide, and none is rejected only by $m_1$ or $m_2$. For determinant truncation the operative channel is therefore the companion's $\Delta_0$; the moments $m_1$ and $m_2$ add reach only for errors that preserve $m_0$.

\subsection{Weight misplaced at fixed total weight}
\label{sec:fixedtotal}
In the doped one-dimensional Hubbard model the regular ($\omega>0$) current spectral function $A_J(\omega)$ is dominated by a mid-infrared/Mott band of total weight $m_0=\langle J^2\rangle$~\cite{Maldague1977,Basov2011}; on the finite ring the charge stiffness is a separate zero-frequency Drude term~\cite{Kohn1964}, absent from $A_J(\omega)$. A wrong reconstruction moves a fraction $f$ of the weight above $6\,t$ into a spurious low-frequency pole at $2.5\,t$, keeping the total fixed, so a total-weight check passes it (Fig.~\ref{fig:invfalsifier}; Lanczos ground state, Haydock/Ritz current poles~\cite{Haydock1972,Lanczos1950}, every moment exact, no sampling). The first moment $m_1=\tfrac{1}{2}\langle[J,[H,J]]\rangle$~\cite{Kohn1964,HohenbergMartin1965} differs from the reconstruction's by up to $38\%$ of $m_1$ at $f=0.5$ and, under the default threshold rule, rejects from $f\approx0.057$. Here the rule takes $z=1.96$, an assumed $2\%$ bias budget and a shot term at $N_s=5\times10^4$ with the local-estimator variance of each estimated operator on the exact $L=12$ ground state (Appendix~\ref{app:estimator}); its blinded calibration covers only the $L=6$ catalog of Sec.~\ref{sec:blind}. Integrated weight cannot see this error: $m_0$ is preserved by construction.

The misplaced weight also moves $m_{-1}=\int_0^\infty A_J(\omega)/\omega\,d\omega=\sum_n|\langle n|J|0\rangle|^2/\omega_n$, whose kernel $1/\omega$ weights low frequencies, unlike the kernel $\omega^k$ of the positive moments. With $P=\sum_j j\,n_j$ the polarization of an open chain and $\hat T$ the hopping (kinetic) energy, the current of our convention is $J=-i[H,P]$, and this optical \textit{f}-sum moment is a ground-state kinetic energy: $m_{-1}=\tfrac12\langle-\hat T\rangle$ on open chains and $\tfrac12\langle-\hat T\rangle-\mathcal D$ on the ring, with $\mathcal D$ the finite-size Drude weight (derived and verified in Appendix~\ref{app:moments}). On this doped ring only $1.4\%$ of $m_0$ lies below $6\,t$, and $m_{-1}=0.324$ is the small difference of $\tfrac12\langle-\hat T\rangle=5.025$ and $\mathcal D=4.701$: the stiffness cancels $94\%$ of the kinetic term. On the ring the negative-order moment $m_{-1}$ therefore moves most in relative terms ($154\%$ against $38\%$ for $m_1$), $4.0\times$ the shift of $m_1$ and $3.3\times$ that of $m_2$ ($47\%$); on the open chain it moves least, with relative slopes $0.233$ for $m_{-1}$ against $0.719$ for $m_1$.

A larger relative shift does not mean earlier detection. Because of the cancellation, a $2\%$ bias on $\tfrac12\langle-\hat T\rangle$ sets $\tau_{-1}=0.198$, which is $61\%$ of $m_{-1}$, whereas $\tau_1$ and $\tau_2$ are $4.3\%$ of $m_1$ and $m_2$. So $m_2$ rejects first ($f\approx0.046$), then $m_1$ ($0.057$), and $m_{-1}$ only at $f\approx0.20$. The open chain gives the same order, and across spurious-pole positions of $0.25$--$4\,t$ the moment $m_{-1}$ rejects first only for poles at $0.25\,t$ and $0.5\,t$, on both geometries (Appendix~\ref{app:moments}). It is thus a complementary check for weight very close to $\omega=0$, not the most sensitive check for this error model. On the ring, $\mathcal D$ requires ground states at twisted flux and is not a same-sample quantity. We claim no novelty for the identity, the textbook \textit{f}-sum~\cite{Kohn1964,Maldague1977}.

\subsection{Single-particle and collective channels}
The construction applies to any diagonal $T=0$ Lehmann measure, and Appendix~\ref{app:illustrations} illustrates it on three more probes, with injected corruptions and no calibrated threshold. For $A(k,\omega)$ of the half-filled $L=12$ ring, the anticommutator first moment, the Hartree centroid, exposes a corruption that flattens the band-centroid dispersion while keeping the Mott gap, the band edges and the per-$k$ total weight. \section{A blinded, pre-registered calibration in simulation}
\label{sec:blind}
On a sealed catalog of $300$ instances the battery rejected every truncation and most spurious features, missed every under-converged Krylov reconstruction, and its false-positive rate depends on the scale of the simulated noise (Table~\ref{tab:blind}).

\emph{Design.} Following blind-analysis and pre-registration practice~\cite{KleinRoodman2005,Nosek2018}, before any instance existed we sealed by SHA-256 hash (\texttt{0e4c3368\ldots}) the battery ($m_0,m_1,m_2$ plus an interval-Hankel condition), the decision rule and its threshold inputs ($N_s$, $z$, $b$; the formula for $\tau_k$ is fixed in the classifier code, not in the hashed text), the error catalog, the primary endpoint, the secondary predictions and the random seed. The rule is the default rule of Sec.~\ref{sec:estimator}: $N_s=5\times10^4$, the per-circuit budget of the companion's \texttt{ibm\_fez} run~\cite{SQDspec2608} (also the largest of our later $L=8$ runs), an assumed bias $b=2\%$, and $\operatorname{Var}(O_k)$ the local-estimator variance on the prepared state (the truncated-subspace ground state for truncations, the exact ground state otherwise). The generator produced $G=300$ instances: $112$ faithful controls and three \emph{algorithmic} error causes, determinant-subspace (SQD) truncation ($56$), under-converged Krylov reconstruction ($71$), and an analytic-continuation artifact adding a spurious spectral feature ($61$); the classifier saw no labels. Every instance uses the current probe $J$ of the doped $L=6$ Hubbard ring ($N_\uparrow=N_\downarrow=2$, sector dimension $225$) at $U/t\in\{3,4,5,6\}$. Truncations keep the top $d=29$--$138$ of the $186$ ground-state configurations by $|\psi_0|^2$ (\mbox{$16$--$74\%$} of the support) and are mild by weight ($75.1$--$98.7\%$ of $|\psi_0|^2$); in four, $J|0_\mathcal{S}\rangle$ has no component inside $\mathcal{S}$ and the reconstruction is empty. Krylov instances are $n_l$-node Lanczos representations, $n_l\in\{1,2,3,4\}$. A spurious feature is a point ($\delta$) atom of weight $2$--$25\%$ of $m_0$ at a frequency uniform between the sector's lowest and highest excitation energies; with probability $1/2$ the measure is rescaled to restore $m_0$, and in this $m_0$-preserving subclass the atom carries \mbox{$2$--$20\%$} of the reconstructed weight. The independent estimate is the oracle estimator of Sec.~\ref{sec:estimator}, drawn independently of $\bar m_k$. Generator and classifier re-verify the hash and refuse to run if it is broken. The seal is self-attested: it carries no third-party timestamp, the blinding is enforced in code, and sealing, generation, classification and scoring ran in a single invocation, so the code predates the seal but is not covered by it; because the run is deterministic given the sealed seed, the seal cannot by itself exclude earlier runs. Departures of the implementation from the sealed text are listed in Appendix~\ref{app:blinddetails}.

\emph{Results by class.} All $56$ truncations were caught, $55$ of them on $m_0$. The Krylov misses follow from Gauss quadrature: an $n_l$-node Lanczos representation reproduces $m_0,\dots,m_{2n_l-1}$ exactly~\cite{GolubWelsch1969,GM2010}, as Proposition~\ref{prop:leak}(ii) states for any subspace that contains the Krylov space. At the shot-only threshold (no bias term) $6$ of the $15$ one-node instances would be rejected on $m_2$, but $5$ of the $56$ exact $n_l\ge2$ instances would also be rejected, on some moment, from noise alone. Two sealed secondary predictions failed: the one-node Krylov case, expected to be caught, was caught $0/15$; the $m_0$-preserving subclass, sealed as designed-blind with low expected detection, was caught $20/34$, an outcome the pre-registration did not anticipate, because the rescaling restores only $m_0$ and still shifts $m_1$ and $m_2$. The other secondary predictions held.

\emph{Sensitivity to the noise model.} The false-positive rate as implemented is low by construction: the simulated estimator is unbiased, its noise has the standard error assumed by the threshold's shot term, and the threshold budgets a $2\%$ bias comparable to the shot term. Redrawing the noise for the $112$ faithful instances gives a modeled rate of $1.3\%$, far below the sealed nominal level of $14.3\%$; one or fewer false positives occur in more than half of the redraws, so the sealed $1/112$ is typical. A realized bias of $+2\%$ ($-2\%$) on all three moments, at the edge of the budget, gives $8.0\%$ ($11.5\%$; the threshold scales with $|\widehat m_k|$); no larger bias was modeled. With the $1.96$-fold noise of the sealed text the same draws give $40/112=35.7\%$ (Wilson $[27.4,44.9]\%$), above the sealed nominal level and typical of that model (expected rate $34.4\%$ over fresh noise draws), and a detection rate of $126/188=67.0\%$ that includes $17$ of the $56$ Krylov reconstructions with $n_l\ge2$, rejected by noise alone. The shot term uses the amplitude-based local-estimator variance, which a device cannot realize. Inflating that variance by a factor $c$, in both the simulated noise and the threshold (sealed draws rescaled by $\sqrt c$), the frozen rule still rejects all $56$ truncations; at $c=4$ it rejects $17/34$ $m_0$-preserving and $22/27$ weight-changing features at a false-positive rate of $15/112$, and at $c=10$ $15/34$ and $19/27$ at $19/112$. These rates come from one rescaled draw; the expected false-positive rates over fresh noise are $7.1\%$ ($c=4$) and $10.7\%$ ($c=10$). The calibration therefore confirms the implementation; robustness to a real estimator bias is untested.

\emph{Shared-state rescoring.} Post hoc, after unblinding, we rescored the $56$ sealed truncations with the shared-state estimator, after a rebuild that reproduces every sealed reconstruction moment, variance and noise draw to rounding error. \section{What a pass implies and what it cannot see}
\label{sec:pass}
\label{sec:christoffel}
\label{sec:detect}
At the order used here, $m_0$--$m_2$, a pass constrains a reconstruction very little: the guaranteed power against weight redistributed among fixed poles is zero and the classical bracket on its cumulative weight is vacuous at the spectral mean, two statements that concern low-order moment checks in general; and the most natural sample-based form of misplaced weight passes the default thresholds.

\subsection{Bracketing a passing reconstruction}
The moments $m_0,\dots,m_{2n}$ of a positive measure bracket its cumulative weight $F(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}A(\omega)\,d\omega$: every positive measure sharing them lies within the Christoffel function $W_n(x)=1/\sum_{j=0}^n p_j(x)^2$ of the orthonormal polynomials, which is also the largest atom a moment-matching measure can place at $x$, and integrated window weights obey a two-sided Markov--Krein bracket of width at most $W_n(c)+W_n(d)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:christoffel} and Proposition~\ref{prop:markovkrein}, classical and re-derived in Appendix~\ref{app:proof})~\cite{GM2010,ShohatTamarkin1943,Akhiezer1965,KreinNudelman1977,KarlinStudden1966}. Optimal two-sided bounds from equilibrium moments were derived early for a related functional, the response to a damped-harmonic perturbation, which gives the broadened shape of a nuclear-resonance line~\cite{Gordon1968}; Stieltjes--Tchebycheff moment theory built photoabsorption profiles from spectral moments~\cite{Langhoff1976}; and matched-moment brackets serve as a posteriori Kolmogorov--Smirnov and Wasserstein bounds for Lanczos spectrum approximation~\cite{ChenTrogdonUbaru2021}. Here they bound a reconstruction under test. On the doped $L=6$, $U/t=8$ current measure (Appendix~\ref{app:proof}), at the default order ($n=1$) the bracket is the one-sided Chebyshev (Cantelli) bound, whose maximum $m_0$ lies at the spectral mean, $0.01\,t$ from the dominant atom: passing places no constraint on the cumulative weight at the peak. Finite tolerances loosen it further: the largest atom that a positive measure with $|m_k-m_k^{\mathrm{true}}|\le\tau_k$ ($k\le2$) can place above the band is $3.2$--$7.7$ times $W_1$ at $\omega=14$--$20\,t$, and $3.6$--$5.7$ times on the $U/t=4$ measure of the blinded catalog. Even with exact moments through $m_8$ the global maximum falls only to $\max_xW_4=0.882$, and at the dominant atom no order can go below its residue $0.778$. The companion shows by example that exact moments through order $2K+1$ coexist with an $O(1)$ reconstruction error~\cite{SQDspec2608}; the Christoffel function makes this distribution-free at the default order, for every measure that passes. Rejection does not involve $W_n$: the looseness limits what passing implies, not the power to reject.

\subsection{Detectability of fixed-pole redistributions}
The limiting error class is the \emph{within-sector redistribution}: a corruption that keeps the retained determinant support and the reconstructed poles and only reweights those poles. Because the support is unchanged, it contributes no leak term to Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-leak}; it is visible only through the moment shifts characterized below. We study this class because no support-based check can signal it; we know of no mechanism by which a sample-based pipeline produces it. Part~(a) of the statement restates the classical Prony/Vandermonde uniqueness of an atomic measure from its moments~\cite{ShohatTamarkin1943,PottsTasche2013}, and part~(b) turns it, with the sharp Chebyshev bound, into a detectability-versus-shot-budget statement.

\begin{theorem}[Detectability and completeness of the moment battery]
\label{thm:detect}\sloppy
Let
\[
A(\omega)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}w_n\,\delta(\omega-\omega_n),\qquad w_n=\sum_{a:\,E_a-E_0=\omega_n}|\langle a|J|0\rangle|^2>0,
\]
be a diagonal $T=0$ Lehmann measure on $N$ \emph{distinct} poles $\{\omega_n\}$ (the frequencies that carry weight), with $w_n$ the total weight of the eigenstates at $\omega_n$, and let a within-sector redistribution $\mathbf{w}\to\mathbf{w}+\bm{\delta}$ ($\bm{\delta}\neq0$, keeping $w_n+\delta_n\ge0$) act on the \emph{same} poles. Write
\[
\mathbf{m}=V\mathbf{w},\quad V_{kn}=\omega_n^{\,k},\qquad \Delta_k=|(V\bm{\delta})_k|=\Big|\sum_n\delta_n\,\omega_n^{\,k}\Big|,\qquad \rho=\|\bm{\delta}\|_1 .
\]
Then $\|\bm{\delta}\|_2\le\rho\le\sqrt{N}\,\|\bm{\delta}\|_2$, and $\rho/2$ is the weight moved when $m_0$ is preserved.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] \emph{(Completeness in exact arithmetic.)} If the redistribution preserves $m_0,\dots,m_K$ with ${K<N-1}$, then $\Delta_j>0$ for at least one $j$ with ${K<j\le N-1}$; quantitatively
\begin{equation}
\max_{K<j\le N-1}\Delta_j\ \ge\ \frac{\sigma_{\min}(V_{0:N-1})}{\sqrt{N-1-K}}\;\|\bm{\delta}\|_2,
\label{eq:detect-sigmamin}
\end{equation}
with $V_{0:N-1}$ the $N\times N$ node-Vandermonde on rows $0,\dots,N-1$ and both sides in one fixed frequency unit. Hence the order-$(N{-}1)$ battery is \emph{complete} for fixed-pole redistributions: no nonzero within-sector redistribution preserves all of $m_0,\dots,m_{N-1}$. If the poles may also move (two measures of at most $N$ atoms each), completeness holds at order $2N-1$ by Prony counting~\cite{ShohatTamarkin1943,PottsTasche2013}. The only alteration invisible to every moment is weight traded among degenerate eigenstates at one frequency, which leaves $A(\omega)$ unchanged and is not an error.
\item[(b)] \emph{(Finite thresholds.)} At $N_s$ shots, with $\operatorname{Var}(O_k)$ the per-shot variance of the estimator operator $O_k$ and $b_k$ the bias budget (Sec.~\ref{sec:estimator}), each moment carries the two-sided threshold $\tau_k=z_{1-\alpha/2}\,[\operatorname{Var}(O_k)/N_s+b_k^2]^{1/2}$, that is, $z_{1-\alpha/2}$ times its noise floor (the square root). We call a given $\bm{\delta}$ \emph{exposed} at order $j$ when its noise-free shift exceeds the threshold, $\Delta_j>\tau_j$; whether a finite-shot test then rejects it is a question of power (Corollary~\ref{cor:power}). Some redistribution preserving $m_0,\dots,m_K$ is exposed at order $K{+}1$ iff $\Delta_{K+1}^{\max}>\tau_{K+1}$, with $\Delta_{K+1}^{\max}$ the linear program~\eqref{eq:wclp}. If rows $0,\dots,K{+}1$ of $V$ have a nontrivial null space (${N-K-2\ge1}$), the smallest $\Delta_{K+1}$ over such redistributions is zero for every size $\rho$ up to a limit set by positivity: the guaranteed power at order $K{+}1$ is zero, however well separated the poles. Two conditions bound exposure: (i) is sufficient for exposure at some order $j\le N-1$; (ii) is necessary for exposure at order $s-1$.
\par\emph{(i) Sufficient.} If $\bm{\delta}$ preserves $m_0,\dots,m_K$, it is exposed at some order $j\le N-1$ whenever the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:detect-sigmamin} exceeds $\max_{K<j\le N-1}\tau_j$.
\par\emph{(ii) Necessary at order $s-1$ (cluster bound).} If, for some $s\ge2$, $\bm{\delta}$ preserves $m_0,\dots,m_{s-2}$ and is supported on poles within an interval of diameter $D$ and midpoint $\omega_c$, then $(V\bm{\delta})_{s-1}=\sum_n\delta_n\,q(\omega_n)$ for every monic $q$ of degree $s-1$, and the Chebyshev choice of $q$ gives the sharp bound $\Delta_{s-1}\le2\rho\,(D/4)^{s-1}$, attained by alternating weights on the Chebyshev extrema of the interval (for $s=1$, when nothing is preserved, the sharp bound is $\Delta_0\le\rho$); every higher shift obeys $\Delta_j\le\rho\sum\nolimits_{i=s-1}^{j}\tbinom{j}{i}|\omega_c|^{j-i}(D/2)^{i}$, which is also $O(\rho D^{s-1})$. Exposure at order $s-1$ therefore requires
\begin{equation}
\rho\,>\,\frac{\tau_{s-1}}{2(D/4)^{s-1}},\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad N_s\,>\,\frac{z_{1-\alpha/2}^{2}\,\operatorname{Var}(O_{s-1})}{4\rho^2(D/4)^{2(s-1)}-z_{1-\alpha/2}^2\,b_{s-1}^2}
\label{eq:detect-Ns}
\end{equation}
when the denominator is positive, and no $N_s$ exposes it at order $s-1$ when $2\rho\,(D/4)^{s-1}\le z_{1-\alpha/2}\,b_{s-1}$; a higher tested order can still expose it, within the term-by-term bound above. The shot cost to detect a redistribution of fixed size at its first exposable order grows at least as $D^{-2(s-1)}$ as the cluster contracts.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\noindent\emph{Proof.} Appendix~\ref{app:withinsector}.

\noindent\emph{Remarks.} Super-resolution bounds for clustered nodes~\cite{BatenkovClustered2020,KunisNagel2021,Donoho1992}, like the separation threshold of Ref.~\cite{Moitra2015}, are proved for Fourier (unit-circle) nodes; for real nodes and power moments, worst-case error bounds of matching order for a near-colliding cluster are given in Ref.~\cite{AkinshinGoldmanYomdin2021}. Part~(b)(ii) relies on none of them. For real nodes the same $\sigma_{\min}\propto D^{s-1}$ scaling, at fixed relative geometry of the cluster, follows from the lower bound on $\sigma_{\min}$ implied by Gautschi's inverse-norm bound~\cite{Gautschi1962,Gautschi1963} and an elementary null-space upper bound, so for an isolated cluster the sufficient condition~(i) weakens with the same power as the necessary bound~(ii) (Appendix~\ref{app:withinsector}, which also gives the shot-cost scaling under uniform cluster contraction). In part~(b), $K$ is the highest \emph{preserved} order and $K{+}1$ the tested one, so a battery through $m_{K+1}$ faces a null space of dimension $N-K-2$; elsewhere in the text $K$ is the highest order of the battery itself (Sec.~\ref{sec:estimator}), and the same null space has dimension $N-K-1$. The fixed-pole class excludes under-converged Krylov reconstructions, whose poles move; their misses are explained in Sec.~\ref{sec:blind}.

\ifdefined\corollary\else\newtheorem{corollary}{Corollary}\fi
\begin{corollary}[Detection power vs.\ cluster size and shot count]
\label{cor:power}
Under a Gaussian approximation to the estimator (for the $5\times10^4$-shot mean of the local estimator on the $U/t=4$ ring the exact skewness is $-0.014$ to $-0.009$ and the excess kurtosis below $5\times10^{-4}$ at orders $k=0,\dots,4$; Appendix~\ref{app:estimator} checks $\widehat m_1$ by Monte Carlo), the test reaches power $1-\beta$ at level $\alpha$ when $\Delta_j\gtrsim z_{\alpha,\beta}\,\tau_j/z_{1-\alpha/2}$, that is, when the shift exceeds $z_{\alpha,\beta}$ noise floors, with $z_{\alpha,\beta}=z_{1-\alpha/2}+z_{1-\beta}$ and the bias budget counted in the noise floor as in the default rule. By Theorem~\ref{thm:detect}(b)(ii) this requires $N_s\gtrsim z_{\alpha,\beta}^2\operatorname{Var}(O_{s-1})/[4\rho^2(D/4)^{2(s-1)}-z_{\alpha,\beta}^2 b_{s-1}^2]$ at the first exposable order $s-1$ ($s\ge2$), and no budget suffices at order $s-1$ below the bias-limited cluster diameter $D_\star=4\,[z_{\alpha,\beta}\,b_{s-1}/(2\rho)]^{1/(s-1)}$. Without bias, and when $\Delta_{s-1}\neq0$ (always so if $\bm\delta$ is supported on exactly $s$ poles), the order-$(s-1)$ cost is finite for every $D>0$ and diverges only as $D\to0$.
\end{corollary}

For the default $L=6$, $U/t=4$ battery the $N=5$ poles are well separated (minimal separation $\Delta\omega_{\min}=0.87\,t$; on nodes rescaled to $[-1,1]$, $\sigma_{\min}(V_{0:4})=0.056$ and $\kappa=50$), yet its guaranteed power against fixed-pole redistribution is zero. The reason is the null-space dimension, not conditioning: rows $0,1,2$ of $V$ leave a two-dimensional null space on five poles, so every redistribution in it passes, whatever its size; the same holds for any spectrum with four or more distinct poles. Over $m_0,m_1$-preserving redistributions the linear program~\eqref{eq:wclp} puts the signed shift of $m_2$ in $[-0.92,7.09]$ (Table~\ref{tab:withinsector}): up to $3.8\,\tau_2$ ($\tau_2=1.848$), but the range contains zero. Table~\ref{tab:counter} lists explicit reconstructions that pass the default battery. Orders $3$ and $4$ would catch some of them, but not all: with the same rule ($\tau_3=15.0$, $\tau_4=122$; the exact-state local-estimator variance behind them is expected to underestimate the true per-shot variance, so realistic thresholds would be wider), over all redistributions that keep $m_0,m_1,m_2$ exactly $\Delta_3\le0.35\,\tau_3$, and $\Delta_4$ reaches $1.19\,\tau_4$ at the one that maximizes $\Delta_3$, a marginal case. Neither $m_3$ nor $m_4$ is estimated in this work. The residual blind spot thus has two sources: the battery order, since with $m_0,\dots,m_K$ every fixed-pole redistribution in the $(N-K-1)$-dimensional null space passes, regardless of pole separation; and pole clustering, which by~\eqref{eq:detect-Ns} raises the shot cost of detection at least as $D^{-2(s-1)}$. Injectivity guarantees that some $m_j$ separates any fixed-pole redistribution; the thresholds decide whether that separation is observable at a given budget.

\subsection{Full-support Rayleigh--Ritz, and non-closure versus error size}
\label{sec:rr}
The natural sample-based form of misplaced weight passes the default thresholds. Rayleigh--Ritz on the full ground-state support of the calibration ring ($186$ determinants at $U/t=4$, so that $|0_\mathcal S\rangle=|0\rangle$) captures all of $J|0\rangle$, so by Proposition~\ref{prop:leak}(iii) $\Delta_0$ and $\Delta_1$ vanish (below $10^{-14}$ of $m_0$ and $m_1$) and the first residual is the boundary coupling at $k=2$: $\Delta_2=0.62$, $1.9\%$ of ${m_2=33.39}$, which is $0.34\,\tau_2$. Under the default thresholds $\tau_k$ the reconstruction passes. Read as a leak score, $\Delta_2/\widehat m_2=0.019$ lies far above the numerical tolerance, so the shared-state test flags it as not closed at $k=2$, but nothing in that score shows that its relative $L_1$ error at $\eta=0.15\,t$ is $0.93$: Rayleigh--Ritz moves the dominant pole from $7.83\,t$ to $7.45\,t$ and the pole at $2.73\,t$ to $4.43\,t$. The pass under $\tau_k$ is set by the assumed bias budget: without it the $k=2$ rule would reject above $N_s\approx2.2\times10^5$ shots, and any tolerance on $m_2$ below $1.9\%$ of $m_2$ rejects it (the default $\tau_2$ is $5.5\%$ of $m_2$). Over $U/t=3$--$8$, $\Delta_2$ is $0.20$--$0.36\,\tau_2$ ($0.95$--$2.2\%$ of $m_2$) and the relative $L_1$ error $0.68$--$0.99$; it passes $\tau_k$ at every $U/t$ (\texttt{src/\allowbreak full\_support\_rr.py}, \texttt{data/\allowbreak 2026-10-02\_full\_support\_rr.json}). This is the companion's no-go at unit captured weight~\cite{SQDspec2608}, now as a reconstruction that passes a thresholded test.

Non-closure is not error size. Over the $176$ truncations of the $U/t=4$ ring with a non-degenerate subspace ground state and a non-empty reconstruction ($7\le d\le185$), the shared-state leak scores $\Delta_k/\widehat m_k$ correlate only moderately with the relative $L_1$ error (Spearman $0.44$, $0.43$ and $0.31$ for $k=0,1,2$), and they are large ($0.38$--$0.46$ at $k=0$) at the mid-coverage truncations that pass $\tau_k$ with exact moments. With the shared-state estimator and $\tau_k$ as a convention, $16$ of the $185$ truncations pass ($d=170$--$185$, $91$--$99.5\%$ coverage, relative $L_1$ errors $0.94$--$1.04$), while the nine exact-moment passes at $d=86$, $87$, $95$ and $160$--$165$ are rejected; read as leak scores against the $10^{-6}$ tolerance, all $185$ are flagged (the $k=2$ score is at least $0.014$). A large leak score shows that a reconstruction is not closed under the full operators; a small one does not show that it is accurate.

\section{Device-sampled supports: coverage is not fidelity}
\label{sec:device}
\suppressfloats[t]%
On supports sampled by \texttt{ibm\_fez}, the coverage residual $\Delta_0$ rewards support spreading, not fidelity: noise lowers it at the largest budget, a sampler with no quantum content does best, and the reconstruction with the smallest residual still has a relative $L_1$ error of $0.35$. On hardware the device supplies only the sampled support $\mathcal S$; every moment in this section is computed classically on it, with exact amplitudes.

\emph{Setup.} We ran four jobs at $L=8$ ($16$ qubits, periodic chain, $U/t=4$; one job per shot budget, job IDs under Data availability) and deposit their raw counts; at $L=6$ even noiseless sampling of the companion's seven circuits, pooled at $5\times10^{4}$ shots each, covers the whole $300$-configuration sector~\cite{SQDspec2608}. The probe is $J=c^{\dagger}_{0\uparrow}$ and the reference state is $|\varphi\rangle=J|0\rangle$, with $|0\rangle$ the exact half-filled ground state ($E_0=-4.6035\,t$); here $\varphi$ is built from the exact ground state, whereas Proposition~\ref{prop:leak} defines it on the subspace state. It lies in the addition sector $N_\uparrow=5$, $N_\downarrow=4$ of dimension $3920$. Its weight is $m_0=\langle J^\dagger J\rangle=\langle c_{0\uparrow}c^{\dagger}_{0\uparrow}\rangle=1-\langle n_{0\uparrow}\rangle=1/2$ exactly, because translation invariance fixes $\langle n_{j\uparrow}\rangle=4/8$ on every site (operator and Lehmann values agree to $6\times10^{-17}$). The seven circuits belong to the companion's family~\cite{SQDspec2608}: each prepares a computational-basis determinant with $X$ gates (five up and four down electrons) and applies three Trotter steps of on-site $R_{zz}$ and nearest-neighbor $R_{xx}R_{yy}$ hopping, without Jordan--Wigner strings, up to times $0,0.5,\dots,3.0$ in units of $1/t$. They conserve particle number and $S_z$, so without noise they sample only the addition sector; they do not apply the Hubbard propagator to $c^{\dagger}_p|0\rangle$ and are not targeted at $|\varphi\rangle$. We ran them at $5\times10^{4}$, $3\times10^{4}$, $1.6\times10^{4}$ and $4\times10^{3}$ shots per circuit (labeled $50$k, $30$k, $16$k and $4$k below; $3.5\times10^{5}$ shots in total at $50$k) with the SamplerV2 primitive of Qiskit Runtime, using measurement twirling, Pauli gate twirling and XpXm dynamical decoupling (pairs of $\pi$ pulses about $+x$ and $-x$ on idle qubits); no readout-error mitigation was applied (SamplerV2 returns twirled raw counts). Each bitstring of the pooled record is mapped to a configuration in Qiskit's bit order (the rightmost bit is qubit $0$) and post-selected to the sector, with no configuration recovery, which keeps $23.5$--$24.0\%$ of the shots ($82{,}697$ of $350{,}000$ at $50$k: $92\%$ of the shots of the zero-time circuit and $11$--$13\%$ of each of the six evolved circuits).

The retained bitstrings define $\mathcal S$, on which the coverage residual $\Delta_0=|m_0-\bar m_0|={\langle\varphi|(\openone-\Pi_\mathcal S)|\varphi\rangle}$ is evaluated exactly. As the budget falls from $50$k to $4$k, the configuration coverage $|\mathcal S|/3920$ falls from $94\%$ to $35\%$ and the residual grows: $\Delta_0=0.0044$, $0.0323$, $0.1232$ and $0.3726$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:device}). In the companion's notation $\Delta_0=m_0(1-w)$, with $w$ the captured weight of $\mathcal S$~\cite{SQDspec2608}; coverage and captured weight differ, and at $50$k $94\%$ of the configurations carry $99.1\%$ of the weight ($1-w=0.009$). The first-moment residual $\Delta_1=|\widehat m_1-\bar m_1|$ ($0.019$, $0.057$, $0.024$, $0.869$) is not monotone in the budget.

\emph{Reference samplers.} We report empirical percentile ranks (the fraction of replicas at or below the device value), not Gaussian $z$-scores, because the weight of $|\varphi\rangle$ is concentrated ($1\%$ of the configurations carry $58\%$ of $m_0$) and the distributions of $\Delta_0$ are skewed. (i) \emph{Noiseless sampling at the device's raw shots.} Two thousand multinomial replicas of the exact output distributions of the same seven circuits give $\Delta_0={0.036\pm0.018}$, ${0.066\pm0.027}$, ${0.121\pm0.035}$ and ${0.269\pm0.032}$ (mean $\pm$ sd) at $50$/$30$/$16$/$4$k. The device lies below all $2000$ replicas at $50$k, at the $1.7$th percentile at $30$k, at the $57.9$th at $16$k and above all $2000$ at $4$k. Its support is larger than in every replica at $50$k and $30$k ($|\mathcal S|=3682$ against ${3379\pm14}$; $3330$ against ${3183\pm16}$) and smaller than in every replica at $16$k and $4$k ($2798$ against ${2877\pm18}$; $1379$ against ${2027\pm21}$). Noiseless sampling cannot reach $\Delta_0=0$: the seven output distributions together cover $3864$ of the $3920$ configurations, leaving $8.8\times10^{-5}$ at infinite shots. The device's $\Delta_1$ lies at the $35$th, $82$nd and $27.5$th percentiles at $50$/$30$/$16$k and above all replicas at $4$k. (ii) \emph{Uniform noise.} Mixing each circuit's output with a fraction $p$ of uniformly random bitstrings changes the expected $\Delta_0$ at $50$k from $0.036$ at $p=0$ to a minimum of $0.0020$ near $p\approx0.93$, and $0.0024$ at $p=1$. At the $p$ that reproduces the device's retention, $p\approx0.81$, the mixture's mean ($0.0023$, $0.017$, $0.075$, $0.295$) lies below the device at every budget, while the device's $\Delta_0$ lies inside the mixture's replica distribution at $50$--$16$k (Appendix~\ref{app:device}). (iii) \emph{Uniform in-sector sampler.} Drawing configurations uniformly from the sector at the device's retained count $N_{\mathrm{ret}}$, with no device and no circuit, gives $\mathbb{E}[\Delta_0]=m_0(1-1/3920)^{N_{\mathrm{ret}}}$, which is $3.4\times10^{-10}$, $1.5\times10^{-6}$, $5.2\times10^{-4}$ and $0.093$, below the device at every budget. At $50$k and $30$k this sampler covers essentially the whole sector, and in all ten replicas checked its reconstruction is exact to the Lanczos floor (relative $L_1$ error below $4\times10^{-9}$). (iv) Noiseless sampling at the device's retained rather than raw counts puts the device below every replica at $50$, $30$ and $16$k (Appendix~\ref{app:device}).

\emph{What the device run shows.} The noisy device leaves less weight uncovered than all $2000$ noiseless replicas of its own circuits at $50$k and than $98\%$ of them at $30$k, where its support is the larger one; at $16$k and $4$k, where its support is smaller, it is typical or worse. Uniform noise lowers $\Delta_0$ at $50$k, and a sampler with no quantum content does best, because the $3920$-configuration sector is small enough for uniform sampling to cover all or most of it at these budgets ($81\%$ of the configurations at $4$k); a related effect has been reported for ground-state quantum-selected configuration interaction, where sampling noise can enlarge the configuration space beyond what the ideal ansatz supports~\cite{VaqueroSabater2026}. $\Delta_0$ grows as the budget falls for every sampler considered. The coverage residual can be evaluated on a device-sampled support; it cannot distinguish a good device from a noisy one and does not certify the reconstruction. This is a practical caution for any study that cites coverage or captured weight as evidence of sampler quality.

\emph{Under the default thresholds, a false negative.} The relative $L_1$ error of each reconstruction on the job's $600$-point frequency grid at $\eta=0.15\,t$, ${\int|A_\mathcal S-A|\,d\omega/\!\int A\,d\omega}$, is $0.35$, $0.56$, $0.90$ and $1.12$ at $50$/$30$/$16$/$4$k. At $50$k both residuals, $\Delta_0=0.0044$ and $\Delta_1=0.019$, lie below the bias terms that the default rule would set ($\tau_k\ge z\,b\,m_k$ with $z=1.96$ and $b=2\%$: $\tau_0\ge0.020$, $\tau_1\ge0.082$), yet the relative $L_1$ error is $0.35$. These thresholds are counterfactual: the rule was calibrated for the $L=6$ current probe, and here $\widehat m_0,\widehat m_1$ are exact. Under the same rule the other three reconstructions, with large $L_1$ errors ($0.56$, $0.90$, $1.12$), would be rejected on $\Delta_0$ ($0.0323$, $0.1232$, $0.3726$), since the shot term cannot raise $\tau_0$ that far ($O_0=J^\dagger J=1-n_{0\uparrow}$ is a projector, with variance at most $1/4$). On these device data the rule would thus reject three poor reconstructions and pass the $50$k one (read at numerical tolerance, as the leak score is, $\Delta_0/m_0=0.009$ already flags it as not closed, which says nothing about the size of its error), a false negative and a device-data instance of the companion's result that captured weight alone cannot certify a reconstruction: its bound places the error above $1-w=0.009$ and needs the boundary coupling for an upper bound~\cite{SQDspec2608} (the companion's own relative $L_1$ error of $0.35$ at $L=8$ refers to a different setting, the probe's full support at $\eta=0.18\,t$). In $L_1$ error the device beats all $80$ noiseless replicas at $50$k and loses to all of them at $4$k, and the uniform in-sector sampler beats the device at every budget (Appendix~\ref{app:device}).

\emph{Complete supports and device-native estimators.} The companion's own $L=6$ \texttt{ibm\_fez} run retained the whole $300$-configuration addition sector, so its reconstruction equals exact diagonalization by coverage; it shows that the pipeline executes, and every determinant it retained arose from a device error after a reversed bit order~\cite{SQDspec2608}. Two earlier $L=6$ jobs of ours (2026-08-21; raw records deposited) likewise retain the whole $300$-configuration sector, under either bit order, so a moment test on them returns the same verdict with or without the device data; they enter no result (Appendix~\ref{app:device}). The discriminating moments $m_1,m_2$ need a device-native estimator, which we have verified only in simulation; its forecast depolarizing bias without mitigation and the diagonal $n_k$ alternative are in Appendix~\ref{app:native}.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:limits}
\label{sec:stack-main}

\subsection{Relation to prior work}
\label{sec:related}
Self-verifying variational simulation certifies a prepared \emph{state} through an energy-variance bound measured on the device~\cite{Kokail2019}; observable-estimation validation bounds the accuracy of error-mitigated \emph{expectation values} by validating the noise model and the mitigation~\cite{OEACV2026}. Our test does neither: it tests a reconstructed spectral \emph{function} for weight misassignment, documented by the companion for sample-based spectral functions~\cite{SQDspec2608}, alongside the limitations documented~\cite{Reinholdt2025,GaberleJattana2026,VaqueroSabater2026} and the noise sensitivity analyzed~\cite{Epperly2022,Kirby2024} for the ground-state energies of subspace methods. Cross-checking the classical diagonalizer across independent LAPACK paths~\cite{CertifyED2026} catches solver errors but not sampling incompleteness or weight misassignment. The approaches are complementary: a hardware spectrum could be produced under observable-estimation validation from a self-verified input state and then tested with our moments. Pipelines that hold the probe acting on the sampled ground state exactly, such as the Green's-function solver of Patel \emph{et al.}~\cite{Patel2026}, never lose spectral weight; for them the coverage channel carries no signal, and only the higher moments test them.

Moment information can instead be built \emph{into} the reconstruction: Nevanlinna continuation imposes the Pick/positivity structure of a causal response~\cite{Fei2021}, maximum-entropy continuation can take moments and sum rules as prior information~\cite{Jarrell1996}, stochastic continuation samples positive spectra at a normalization fixed by the data~\cite{Sandvik1998}, stochastic pole expansion enforces the zeroth-moment sum rule on its pole weights and admits further prior constraints on pole positions and amplitudes~\cite{HuangLiang2023}, and explicit pole representations such as minimal-pole fits~\cite{ZhangGull2024} could carry further linear constraints pinning low-order moments to their operator values. Where available this is preferable, but such a spectrum is consistent only with the moments \emph{it} was given, within the method that enforced them, for the data it was built from. Irmejs and Santos propose building dynamical correlation functions on hardware without real-time evolution, from weight-$O(k)$ moments measured on a prepared eigenstate, prove exponential accuracy away from the real axis, and demonstrate the method in classical simulations of small lattices~\cite{IrmejsSantos2025}; a circuit-differentiation framework builds the retarded Green's function from derivatives of real-time-evolution circuits under realistic noise~\cite{Piccinelli2026}; classical shadows~\cite{HuangKuengPreskill2020,HuangScience2022} could supply device-side estimates of $\langle O_k\rangle$, their spectral specialization extracts gaps without forming $A(\omega)$~\cite{ShadowSpec2025}, and their fermionic variant constructs dynamical correlation functions~\cite{FAST2025}. These programs construct the spectrum. Our test is their diagnostic counterpart for a user who holds the pipeline's sampled support $\mathcal S$ (and hence $|0_\mathcal S\rangle$), or an independent estimate of the moments, even if the reconstruction itself was produced elsewhere, on the real axis, where the constructive guarantees are weakest. Semidefinite moment bounds~\cite{WangAcin2024,Mortimer2026} applied to the operators $O_k$ would bound the true moments, and membership of the read-off moments in such intervals would be a second necessary condition of the same logical type as ours (Appendix~\ref{app:stack}). No finite set of moment conditions certifies $A(\omega)$.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitlist}
\emph{Necessary, not sufficient.} Unless the moments lie on the boundary of the moment body (singular Hankel matrix: fewer poles than the moments resolve), infinitely many positive measures share a finite set of moments, and within any nonzero tolerance they always do, so passing can never certify correctness~\cite{ShohatTamarkin1943,Akhiezer1965}. Section~\ref{sec:pass} makes this quantitative at the order used here: zero guaranteed power against fixed-pole redistributions, a bracket vacuous at the mean, and reconstructions that pass the default thresholds with relative $L_1$ errors of $0.68$--$0.99$ (full-support Rayleigh--Ritz, $U/t=3$--$8$) and up to $1.26$ (truncations that pass with exact moments; \texttt{data/\allowbreak 2026-10-02\_full\_support\_rr.json}). A passing test does not resolve a line shape~\cite{KPM2006,GM2010,Szego1975}.

\emph{The estimator.} The realizable estimator, $\widehat m_k=\langle 0_\mathcal S|O_k|0_\mathcal S\rangle$, is deterministic, and its residual is the leak, not the distance to the true moment: at $L=24$ its $\widehat m_0$ lies $10$--$15.5\%$ below the DMRG $m_0$ (Appendix~\ref{app:L24}). Its tolerance is numerical, and the default $\tau_k$ apply to it only by convention. The rejection rates with shot-noise thresholds come from an oracle estimator, the exact moment plus unbiased Gaussian noise; the $2\%$ bias budget is assumed, not measured, so a rejection is attributed to the reconstruction only under that assumption, and the false-positive rate depends on the simulated noise scale ($1/112$ or $40/112$; Sec.~\ref{sec:blind}). Same-shot estimation of both branches has been realized only in simulation, with a local estimator that needs exact amplitudes (Appendix~\ref{app:estimator}).

\emph{Error classes.} For determinant truncation the operative channel is $m_0$, the companion's captured-weight deficit (Sec.~\ref{sec:detectsim}). Under-converged Krylov reconstructions with two or more Lanczos nodes reproduce $m_0$--$m_2$ and cannot be detected at this order; the one-node case misses $m_2$ by $m_0\operatorname{Var}_A$, below $\tau_2$ on the catalog spectra but not small in general ($36\%$ for the current spectrum of the open $L=12$ chain of Appendix~\ref{app:moments}). That error class calls for a separate instrument, most naturally a Ritz-residual check on the Krylov basis. Neither the moment test nor the Gauss-law check is a general device-noise diagnostic; that is the domain of observable-estimation validation~\cite{OEACV2026}.

\emph{Synthetic errors and scope.} Every error rejected here is synthetic: an injected corruption or a controlled truncation of otherwise exact data, with the truth classically known and withheld only from the classifier. The calibrated scope is the current probe of the doped $L=6$ ring at $U/t\in\{3,4,5,6\}$, truncations keeping $15.6$--$74.2\%$ of the support configurations but $75.1$--$98.7\%$ of the ground-state weight, and spurious point atoms carrying $2$--$25\%$ of $m_0$, modeled as analytic-continuation artifacts whose realism for sample-based pipelines is not established. The single-particle and collective cases are illustrations without calibrated thresholds. No error has yet been caught in a spectrum whose correctness was unknown in advance.

\emph{Hardware.} No moment has been estimated from device counts. For a surrogate bond-basis probe, without error mitigation, the forecast depolarizing bias of $m_1$ at $295$ CZ and two-qubit error $2.5\times10^{-3}$ is $38\%$ of the signal under a static local noise model and $116\%$ under global depolarizing noise; the global part can be removed by noise renormalization once its strength is calibrated~\cite{Vovrosh2021,Kiss2025}, and the depth of the ground-state preparation that the estimator needs is not established (Appendix~\ref{app:native}). The sealed on-device $n_k$ test has not been run: on the calibrations checked (2026-09-03 to 2026-09-05) no $12$-qubit chain met its sealed coherence floors, and no job was submitted (Appendix~\ref{app:native}).

\emph{Scale.} One-dimensional Hubbard rings at $L\le 12$ are solved outright by DMRG and exact diagonalization, so no quantum resource is needed for their spectra. This is deliberate: calibration requires exact moments, and the test is characterized against these methods, not in competition with them. Its intended regime, where no controlled classical spectral reference exists, is real-time dynamics of two-dimensional and frustrated lattices and \emph{ab initio} molecular spectra. The only run beyond exact diagonalization is a code-scalability check (Appendix~\ref{app:L24}).

\emph{Positivity.} The positivity that underwrites the moment bounds is that of a diagonal $T=0$ Lehmann measure~\cite{Lehmann1954}. Off-diagonal spectral functions are not positive measures in general. At finite temperature the diagonal spectral function stays positive, but its support extends below $\omega=0$ and its moments are thermal averages rather than ground-state expectation values, so the estimator and the bounds used here need separate treatment.

\subsection{Outlook}
\label{sec:outlook}
A device-side use of the test needs the mitigation bias measured and bounded rather than assumed, for example from error-model violation~\cite{Govia2025} or by validating the noise model and the mitigation~\cite{OEACV2026}. Hamiltonian moments of small Hubbard and impurity models have been measured on trapped ions~\cite{GreeneDiniz2024}, and related quantities on IBM hardware~\cite{Vallury2023,Leblanc2026}; estimating $m_1,m_2$ for the probes here with the bond-basis estimator awaits a constructed ground-state preparation and calibrated mitigation or lower gate error. The Gauss-law check, evaluated here exactly rather than from samples, belongs with symmetry verification~\cite{BonetMonroig2018,McArdleYuanBenjamin2019}, Gauss-law oracles~\cite{Stryker2019} and sum-rule checks~\cite{TurkowskiFreericks2006,Freericks2009}: exactly known identities, each testing a different error class~\cite{Gauss2020}. Extending the test to off-diagonal spectra, where positivity fails, and to finite temperature, where the support extends below $\omega=0$ and the estimator must target thermal moments, is the main theoretical opening. Built on kernel-polynomial, recursion and quadrature machinery~\cite{KPM2006,Haydock1972,GM2010}, the test attaches to sample-based diagonalization and Krylov pipelines developed for ground states~\cite{SQDchem2025,QSCI2023,SKQD2501,SqDRIFT2508,QKDHub2607} and extended to the spectra it tests~\cite{SQDspec2608,Patel2026}.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}
Checking a sample-based spectral function against moments read off itself is circular; checking it against full-operator moments on the subspace ground state that the pipeline holds is not. The difference between the two is exactly the leak of the probe state out of the subspace, order by order. The leak is the missed weight at $k=0$ and the squared boundary coupling at $k=2$ when no weight is missed. It vanishes through order $2K+1$ on a subspace that contains the order-$K$ Krylov space of the probe state. A rejection by this shared-state estimator shows that the reconstruction is not closed under the full operators, not how far a moment is from the truth. Read against a numerical tolerance, its residual flags almost every sampled support and does not track the error. The detection and false-positive rates below therefore use the oracle estimator: exact moments plus shot noise.

The operator form $J^\dagger(H-e_0)^kJ$ is what isolates the leak. Off an eigenstate and at the default threshold, a lone first-moment check in the textbook commutator form passes a reconstruction whose first moment is $11\%$ low.

Truncation is the native error of sample-based diagonalization. Its operative channel is the zeroth-order leak, which is the companion study's captured-weight deficit. The first and second moments reach the error that captured weight cannot see: weight moved in frequency at fixed total weight. In exact simulation they reject it from a moved fraction of a few percent.

In a pre-registered blinded simulation with the oracle estimator, the battery rejected all $56$ truncations and $45$ of $61$ spurious features ($74\%$), including $20$ of the $34$ that preserve the total weight, but none of the $71$ under-converged Krylov reconstructions. Its false-positive rate was $1/112$ with the simulated noise the threshold assumes. With the $1.96$-fold larger noise the pre-registration specified, the rate was $40/112$, and noise also rejected $23$ Krylov reconstructions, $17$ of them exact through $m_2$.

Low-order moment checks have proven limits. With $m_0$--$m_2$, the guaranteed power against fixed-pole weight redistributions is zero for any spectrum with four or more distinct poles, and the cumulative-weight bracket is vacuous at the spectral mean. These two limits apply to every sum-rule validation at this order.

Rayleigh--Ritz on the full ground-state support of the calibration ring has a relative $L_1$ error of $0.93$. It passes the default thresholds and would pass any tolerance on $m_2$ above $1.9\%$ of $m_2$. Its leak score flags it as not closed but does not measure that error.

On \texttt{ibm\_fez}-sampled supports, with every moment evaluated classically, the coverage residual grows from $0.0044$ to $0.37$ as coverage falls from $94\%$ to $35\%$. It rewards support spreading rather than device fidelity. A uniform in-sector sampler leaves less weight uncovered than the device at every budget, and the reconstruction with the smallest residual has a relative $L_1$ error of $0.35$.

Five problems remain open. The first is estimating $m_1$ and $m_2$ on hardware, where the forecast bias for a surrogate probe is $38$--$116\%$ of the signal without mitigation. The second is calibrating the test with a measured mitigation bias and a same-sample estimator realized on a device. The third is closing the blind spot for fixed-pole redistributions with higher moments at an affordable shot cost. The fourth is applying the semidefinite moment bounds of Refs.~\cite{WangAcin2024,Mortimer2026} to the same moment sequence. The last is a field test on a spectrum whose correctness is not known in advance.

\section{Derivation of the moment identities}
\label{app:moments}
We derive the operator form of the spectral moments used throughout. Each $m_k$ is a ground-state expectation value computable without the dynamics, and we state where the derivation needs an exact eigenstate. Let $H=H^\dagger$ have a complete orthonormal eigenbasis $\{|n\rangle\}$, ${H|n\rangle=E_n|n\rangle}$, and let $|0\rangle$ be an eigenstate of lowest energy $E_0$. The derivation uses only that $|0\rangle$ is an eigenstate. If the lowest level is degenerate, $A(\omega)$ and its moments refer to the chosen member; for the $L=6$, $U/t=8$ current measure, whose lowest level is a spin triplet, the measure is the same for all three members because $J$ is a spin scalar (Appendix~\ref{app:evaluate}). The probe $J$ (a current or density) is Hermitian unless stated otherwise; we keep $J^\dagger$ on the bra side so that the same steps also cover the non-Hermitian probes treated below. The diagonal zero-temperature Lehmann spectral function
\begin{equation}
A(\omega)=\sum_n |\langle n|J|0\rangle|^2\,\delta\!\big(\omega-(E_n-E_0)\big)
\label{eq:app-lehmann}
\end{equation}
is a non-negative measure supported on $\omega=E_n-E_0\ge 0$, and its power moments are
\[
m_k=\int\omega^k A(\omega)\,d\omega=\sum_n(E_n-E_0)^k\,|\langle n|J|0\rangle|^2 .
\]

Writing $|\langle n|J|0\rangle|^2=\langle 0|J^\dagger|n\rangle\langle n|J|0\rangle$, using $\langle n|(H-E_0)^k=(E_n-E_0)^k\langle n|$, and closing with completeness $\sum_n|n\rangle\langle n|=\openone$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
m_k&=\sum_n\langle 0|J^\dagger|n\rangle(E_n-E_0)^k\langle n|J|0\rangle\\
&=\langle 0|\,J^\dagger(H-E_0)^kJ\,|0\rangle=\langle 0|O_k|0\rangle ,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:app-power}
\end{equation}
where
\[
O_k\equiv J^\dagger(H-e_0)^kJ,\qquad e_0=\langle H\rangle ,
\]
is the moment operator of the estimator, with $e_0$ the energy of the state on which it is evaluated ($e_0=E_0$ on the exact eigenstate $|0\rangle$). Because $|0\rangle$ is annihilated by $H-E_0$, acting to the right gives $(H-E_0)\,J|0\rangle=[H,J]\,|0\rangle$, and iterating,
\begin{equation}
(H-E_0)^k J|0\rangle=\operatorname{ad}_H^{\,k}(J)\,|0\rangle,\qquad \operatorname{ad}_H(J)\equiv[H,J],
\label{eq:app-adjoint}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{ad}_H^{\,k}$ denotes the $k$-fold nested commutator $[H,[H,\cdots[H,J]]]$. Substituting Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-adjoint} into Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-power} gives the commutator form
\begin{equation}
m_k=\langle 0|\,J^\dagger\operatorname{ad}_H^{\,k}(J)\,|0\rangle=\langle 0|C_k|0\rangle,\qquad C_k\equiv J^\dagger\operatorname{ad}_H^{\,k}(J).
\label{eq:app-commutator}
\end{equation}
Both $O_k$ and $C_k$ are built solely from $H$ and $J$, with no reference to the spectrum or to the reconstructed $A(\omega)$, and $\langle C_k\rangle=\langle O_k\rangle=m_k$ on an exact eigenstate; off an eigenstate they differ (below).

The lowest moments specialize immediately. For $k=0$, $O_0=C_0=J^\dagger J$ gives the total weight $m_0=\langle J^\dagger J\rangle$, which is $\langle J^2\rangle$ for a Hermitian probe. For $k=1$ and $J=J^\dagger$, ${m_1=\langle 0|J[H,J]|0\rangle}$; expanding the symmetric double commutator ${[J,[H,J]]}$\allowbreak${{}=2JHJ-J^2H-HJ^2}$ and using $H|0\rangle=E_0|0\rangle$,
\begin{equation}
\langle 0|[J,[H,J]]|0\rangle=2\langle 0|J(H-E_0)J|0\rangle=2m_1,
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}
m_1=\tfrac{1}{2}\langle 0|[J,[H,J]]|0\rangle .
\label{eq:app-m1}
\end{equation}
This is the first-moment (double-commutator) sum rule of $A_J$; for the current operator $J$ it fixes the mean frequency of the spectral weight. It is distinct from the optical-conductivity \textit{f}-sum rule $\int_0^\infty\sigma_1\,d\omega=\tfrac{\pi}{2}\langle-\hat T\rangle$ (a ground-state kinetic energy), whose regular part is $\pi m_{-1}$: all of it on open chains, while on the ring the Drude weight takes the rest (see below)~\cite{Kohn1964,Maldague1977,HohenbergMartin1965}. On an exact eigenstate the symmetrized form~\eqref{eq:app-m1} is manifestly real and independent of the additive constant $E_0$, which the one-sided expression $\langle 0|J[H,J]|0\rangle$ obscures.

\emph{Negative-order moment.} The negative-order moment ${m_{-1}=\int_0^\infty A_J(\omega)/\omega\,d\omega}$ of Fig.~\ref{fig:invfalsifier} carries the kernel $1/\omega$ and weights low frequencies; on the ring of that figure it rejects after $m_1$ and $m_2$. It carries the regular part of the optical \textit{f}-sum, $\int_0^\infty\!\mathrm{Re}\,\sigma_{\mathrm{reg}}\,d\omega=\pi m_{-1}$, and it too is a ground-state expectation: since the current is the polarization velocity, $J=-i[H,P]$ with $P=\sum_j j\,n_j$ and $\hat T$ the hopping term of $H$ (the sign is our convention for $J$ and does not enter moments quadratic in $J$), $m_{-1}=\tfrac12\langle[P,[H,P]]\rangle=\tfrac{1}{2}\langle-\hat T\rangle$, a kinetic energy~\cite{Kohn1964,Maldague1977,HohenbergMartin1965}. On open chains we verify this identity against the Lehmann sum over the computed poles, to a relative $2\times10^{-15}$ at $L=6$ and $8$ and $2\times10^{-14}$ at $L=12$. On the ring the polarization is multivalued and the identity acquires Kohn's stiffness correction, $m_{-1}=\tfrac12\langle-\hat T\rangle-\mathcal D$ with $\mathcal D=\tfrac12\,\partial^2_\theta E_0|_{\theta=0}$ the finite-size Drude weight, for the Peierls phase $t\to t\,e^{i\theta}$ on every bond (per-bond, not total, flux); the identity is verified at $L=12$ to a relative $2\times10^{-6}$, with $\mathcal D$ from the flux curvature. The open chain ($\mathcal D=0$) does not restore an advantage for the corruption of Fig.~\ref{fig:invfalsifier}, because $41\%$ of its regular weight already lies below $6\,t$ and $m_{-1}=4.81$ is large: there the thresholds are crossed at $f\approx0.17$ for $m_{-1}$, $0.059$ for $m_1$ and $0.045$ for $m_2$. When the spurious pole is moved across $0.25$--$4\,t$, $m_{-1}$ rejects first only for poles at $0.25\,t$ and $0.5\,t$, on both geometries (at $0.25\,t$ on the ring, $f\approx0.015$ against $0.044$ for $m_1$ and $0.043$ for $m_2$). On open chains $m_{-1}$ is also cheap: $\langle-\hat T\rangle$ is a one-body operator, measured in two qubit-wise-commuting settings that are a subset of those the $m_0,m_1$ estimator already requires (Appendix~\ref{app:native}).

\emph{Off an eigenstate the two forms differ, and the choice matters.} Equations~\eqref{eq:app-power}, \eqref{eq:app-commutator} and~\eqref{eq:app-m1} agree because $|0\rangle$ is an exact eigenstate of $H$. On any other state $|\psi\rangle$, with ${e_0=\langle\psi|H|\psi\rangle}$, for example the ground state $|0_\mathcal{S}\rangle$ of a truncated subspace Hamiltonian (Appendix~\ref{app:estimator}), $\langle\psi|O_k|\psi\rangle$ and $\langle\psi|C_k|\psi\rangle$ are no longer equal for $k\ge1$, and in general neither equals the exact moment $m_k$. At $k=1$, $C_1-O_1=-J^\dagger J\,(H-e_0)$, so
\[
\langle\psi|C_1|\psi\rangle-\langle\psi|O_1|\psi\rangle=-\langle\psi|J^\dagger J\,(H-e_0)|\psi\rangle ,
\]
a term set by the state's own residual $(H-e_0)|\psi\rangle$, which vanishes on an eigenstate (for Hermitian $J$ the real part of $\langle\psi|C_1|\psi\rangle$ is $\tfrac12\langle\psi|[J,[H,J]]|\psi\rangle$). At $k=0$ the two forms coincide.

We evaluate $O_k$, for a structural reason (Appendix~\ref{app:estimator}). With $O_k$ and the reference $e_0$ shared with the reconstruction, the residual $\widehat m_k-\bar m_k$ between the estimate and the reconstruction moment is exactly the operator leak of Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-leak}, the part of the probe state that $H$ carries outside the retained subspace: it vanishes whenever that subspace contains enough of the Krylov space of the probe state, and at $k=0$ it is the companion's captured-weight deficit $\widehat m_0(1-w)$~\cite{SQDspec2608}. With $C_k$ the residual would also contain the state-residual term above, which need not vanish in that case, so it would not isolate the leak. The sealed blinded test of Sec.~\ref{sec:blind} does not depend on this choice: its estimate is the exact moment plus Gaussian noise (its sealed shot widths are local variances of $J^\dagger(H-E_0)^kJ$ on the prepared state, \texttt{src/\allowbreak blind\_generate.py}). The shared-state analysis of Fig.~\ref{fig:teethshared} uses $O_k$ (\texttt{data/\allowbreak 2026-08-26\_teeth\_shared\_state.json}). The post hoc shared-state rescoring of the $56$ sealed truncations (Sec.~\ref{sec:blind}) does not depend on the choice either, because every one of them is rejected at $k=0$, where $O_0=C_0$.

The following example shows that the choice matters (script \texttt{src/\allowbreak estimator\_form\_\allowbreak offeigenstate.py}, output in \texttt{data/\allowbreak 2026-09-27\_theory\_numerics.json}). Take the current probe of the doped $L=6$, $U/t=4$ ring and truncate its sector to the $d=100$ determinants of largest $|\psi_0|^2$ ($44\%$ of the $225$-dimensional sector, $54\%$ of the $186$-determinant ground-state support). With the determinants ordered deterministically (descending $|\psi_0|^2$, ties broken by sector index, NumPy \texttt{lexsort}), the subspace ground state gives $\langle O_1\rangle=4.558$ but $\tfrac12\langle[J,[H,J]]\rangle=3.717$, against the reconstruction moment $\bar m_1=3.743$ and the exact $m_1=4.227$. At the default threshold $\tau_1=0.229$ (Appendix~\ref{app:estimator}) the $O_1$ form rejects the truncated reconstruction and the commutator form does not; with the truth known here, the rejection is the correct verdict, since $\bar m_1$ is $11\%$ below the exact value. At the six other cuts of the same scan ($d=30$, $50$, $75$, $150$, $186$ and $200$) the two forms give the same verdict. This cut falls inside a $24$-fold degenerate shell of $|\psi_0|^2$, so these values depend on how ties are broken: NumPy \texttt{argsort}, which does not fix the order of ties, gives $4.605$, $3.707$ and $3.810$ in the deposited run, with the same two verdicts. The $m_0$ comparison, common to both forms, rejects this cut ($\Delta_0=12\,\tau_0$). In sum, the estimator is $\widehat m_k=\langle\psi|O_k|\psi\rangle$ with $e_0=\langle\psi|H|\psi\rangle$ on the prepared state, and the commutator forms are used only on exact eigenstates.

Two features of these identities make them the operational core of the test. First, $O_k$ is an explicit operator polynomial in $H$ and $J$; mapped to qubits it is a weighted sum of Pauli strings, whose diagonal terms are read from computational-basis counts and whose hopping terms need rotated bases (Appendix~\ref{app:native}). Its expectation can therefore be evaluated on a prepared state without forming $A(\omega)$; this is the estimator $\widehat{m}_k$ of Appendix~\ref{app:estimator}. Besides the idealized evaluation on the exact ground state, it is evaluated here in three ways, none on hardware: classically on the subspace state that also yields the reconstruction (Fig.~\ref{fig:teethshared}, a deterministic $|\psi_0|^2$ truncation; the $L=24$ DMRG test of Appendix~\ref{app:L24}, a shot-sampled subspace); as a local estimator from the same simulated shots, which needs the exact amplitudes (joint sampling, Appendix~\ref{app:estimator}); and from simulated Pauli-grouped measurements of a separately prepared ground state (Appendix~\ref{app:native}). Second, the derivation used only completeness of the eigenbasis and that $|0\rangle$ is an exact eigenstate, so Eqs.~\eqref{eq:app-power}--\eqref{eq:app-commutator} hold for any probe and Hamiltonian, which is why the test transports across models. Positivity of the measure~\eqref{eq:app-lehmann} is not needed for the identities; it enters only in the positivity conditions and the bounds built on them.

Because the steps above never used $J=J^\dagger$, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:app-power}--\eqref{eq:app-commutator} hold unchanged for a non-Hermitian probe, with $m_0=\langle J^\dagger J\rangle$; only the symmetrized form~\eqref{eq:app-m1} needs a Hermitian $J$. The single-particle spectral function $A_{k\sigma}(\omega)$ of Fig.~\ref{fig:akw} is the two-sided (photoemission plus inverse-photoemission) spectrum of the fermionic probe $c_{k\sigma}$, and its moments close under \emph{anticommutators} rather than the double commutator:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\int A_{k\sigma}\,d\omega&=\big\langle\{c_{k\sigma},c_{k\sigma}^\dagger\}\big\rangle=1,\\
\int \omega\,A_{k\sigma}\,d\omega&=\big\langle\{[c_{k\sigma},H],c_{k\sigma}^\dagger\}\big\rangle=\epsilon_k+U\langle n_{-\sigma}\rangle,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:app-anticomm}
\end{equation}
the Hartree-shifted band centroid~\cite{Freericks2009}. The Hermitian double-commutator map above is the special case $J=J^\dagger$; Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-anticomm} is the version used for the single-particle test of Fig.~\ref{fig:akw}, and it is likewise a ground-state expectation of explicit operators.

\emph{Collective probes.} For the non-Hermitian probes $\rho_q$ and $S^z_q$ of Fig.~\ref{fig:collective_screen} the double commutator is the symmetrized form $\tfrac12[m_1(q)+m_1(-q)]$, equal to $m_1(q)$ by the inversion symmetry of the ring; both forms are evaluated on the exact ground state, where they coincide. Only the kinetic term contributes to $m_1$, since the Hubbard interaction is diagonal in the occupation basis and commutes with $\rho_q$ and $S^z_q$.

\emph{Derivation of the anticommutator moment identities.} Equation~\eqref{eq:app-anticomm} is not a fresh sum rule but the non-Hermitian specialization of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:app-power}--\eqref{eq:app-commutator}, derived here for completeness. Write $d\equiv c_{k\sigma}$, $d^\dagger\equiv c_{k\sigma}^\dagger$, with $\{|n\rangle\}$ the complete eigenbasis of $H$ over all particle-number sectors, $H|n\rangle=E_n|n\rangle$, ground state $|0\rangle$, energy $E_0$. The two-sided single-particle spectral function is the sum of an electron-addition ($N{+}1$) and an electron-removal ($N{-}1$) branch,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
A_{k\sigma}(\omega)={}&\sum_n|\langle n|d^\dagger|0\rangle|^2\,\delta\!\big(\omega-(E_n-E_0)\big)\\
&+\sum_n|\langle n|d|0\rangle|^2\,\delta\!\big(\omega+(E_n-E_0)\big),
\end{split}
\label{eq:app-twosided}
\end{equation}
a non-negative measure with addition poles at $\omega=E^{N+1}_n-E_0$ and removal poles at $\omega=E_0-E^{N-1}_n$. Its support \emph{straddles} $\omega=0$ once frequencies are measured from a chemical potential $\mu$ in the gap, that is with $H$ replaced by $H-\mu\hat N$: the addition poles are then positive and the removal poles negative (in the raw-$H$ frame removal poles can be positive), and the identities below hold unchanged in form, with $\mu_1$ shifted by $-\mu$. Its power moments split accordingly,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mu_p\equiv\int\omega^p A_{k\sigma}\,d\omega={}&\sum_n(E_n-E_0)^p|\langle n|d^\dagger|0\rangle|^2\\
&+(-1)^p\sum_n(E_n-E_0)^p|\langle n|d|0\rangle|^2 .
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Applying to each branch the steps of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:app-power}--\eqref{eq:app-commutator}, namely completeness together with ${(E_n-E_0)^p\langle n|X|0\rangle}$\allowbreak${{}=\langle n|\operatorname{ad}_H^{\,p}(X)|0\rangle}$ for $X=d^\dagger$ and $X=d$, gives the closed operator form, the commutator form $C_p$ of each branch probe:
\begin{equation}
\mu_p=\langle 0|\,d\,\operatorname{ad}_H^{\,p}(d^\dagger)\,|0\rangle+(-1)^p\langle 0|\,d^\dagger\,\operatorname{ad}_H^{\,p}(d)\,|0\rangle,
\label{eq:app-twosided-moment}
\end{equation}
built solely from $H$, $d$, and $d^\dagger$. For $p=0$ this is the canonical anticommutator,
\[
\mu_0=\langle 0|\,dd^\dagger+d^\dagger d\,|0\rangle=\langle\{c_{k\sigma},c_{k\sigma}^\dagger\}\rangle=1 .
\]
For $p=1$, using $H|0\rangle=E_0|0\rangle$ and $\langle 0|H=E_0\langle 0|$ on the outer factors one verifies term by term that
\begin{equation}
\mu_1=\langle 0|\,d[H,d^\dagger]\,|0\rangle-\langle 0|\,d^\dagger[H,d]\,|0\rangle=\big\langle\{[c_{k\sigma},H],c_{k\sigma}^\dagger\}\big\rangle,
\label{eq:app-anticomm-m1}
\end{equation}
the anticommutator first-moment identity, manifestly real and independent of the additive constant $E_0$. For the Hubbard Hamiltonian $H=H_0+H_U$ with
\[
H_0=\sum_{k'\sigma'}\epsilon_{k'}\,c^\dagger_{k'\sigma'}c_{k'\sigma'},\qquad H_U=U\sum_i n_{i\uparrow}n_{i\downarrow},
\]
and $c_{k\sigma}=L^{-1/2}\sum_j e^{-ikj}c_{j\sigma}$, the commutators evaluate in closed form,
\[
[c_{k\sigma},H_0]=\epsilon_k\,c_{k\sigma},\qquad [c_{k\sigma},H_U]=\frac{U}{\sqrt L}\sum_j e^{-ikj}c_{j\sigma}n_{j,-\sigma},
\]
and since $\{c_{j\sigma}n_{j,-\sigma},c_{j'\sigma}^\dagger\}=\delta_{jj'}n_{j,-\sigma}$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\big\langle\{[c_{k\sigma},H],c_{k\sigma}^\dagger\}\big\rangle&=\epsilon_k\,\langle\{c_{k\sigma},c_{k\sigma}^\dagger\}\rangle+\frac{U}{L}\sum_j\langle n_{j,-\sigma}\rangle\\
&=\epsilon_k+U\langle n_{-\sigma}\rangle,
\end{split}
\end{equation}
which is the centroid quoted in Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-anticomm} and evaluated per momentum in Fig.~\ref{fig:akw}.

\emph{Scope: which checks transport to the single-particle channel.} The zeroth- and first-moment checks carry over intact: $\mu_0$ and $\mu_1$ are ground-state expectation values of explicit operators [Eqs.~\eqref{eq:app-twosided-moment}--\eqref{eq:app-anticomm-m1}], so they give reconstruction-independent residuals $\Delta_0,\Delta_1$ at each $k$. So does the two-sided Hamburger--Hankel positivity $H_n=[\mu_{i+j}]\succeq 0$, since a positive measure on $\mathbb{R}$ has a positive semidefinite Hankel matrix whatever the sign of its support; it tests the estimated moments, not a non-negative reconstruction (Appendix~\ref{app:estimator}). The shifted-Stieltjes condition $H_n^{(1)}=[\mu_{i+j+1}]\succeq 0$ does \emph{not} transport: it presupposes support on $[0,\infty)$, and $A_{k\sigma}$ is supported on both signs of $\omega$. The Christoffel bracket of Appendix~\ref{app:proof} applies to any positive measure on $\mathbb{R}$, but we have evaluated it only for the one-sided current measure and do not deploy it here. The single-particle test is therefore $\Delta_0,\Delta_1$ and Hamburger--Hankel positivity; the shifted-Stieltjes condition belongs to the one-sided current and density responses.
\section{The reconstruction-independent moment estimator}
\label{app:estimator}
We give the explicit estimator $\widehat{m}_k$ and show why it is independent of the reconstruction and, unlike the spectral moment $\bar m_k$, detects subspace truncation. Sample-based diagonalization returns a set $\mathcal{S}$ of computational-basis determinants $\{|x\rangle\}$ and diagonalizes the projected Hamiltonian $H_\mathcal{S}=\Pi_\mathcal{S} H\Pi_\mathcal{S}$, with $\Pi_\mathcal{S}=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{S}}|x\rangle\langle x|$, on a classical computer~\cite{SQDchem2025,QSCI2023}. This gives eigenpairs $\{E_n^{\mathcal{S}},|n_{\mathcal{S}}\rangle\}$, among them the subspace ground state $|0_\mathcal{S}\rangle=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{S}}c_x|x\rangle$ with energy ${e_0=\langle 0_\mathcal{S}|H|0_\mathcal{S}\rangle}$. Unless $\mathcal{S}$ contains the support of the exact ground state, $|0_\mathcal{S}\rangle$ is an eigenstate of $H_\mathcal{S}$ but in general not of $H$, so $O_k$ and $C_k$ of Appendix~\ref{app:moments} differ on it; we use $O_k$, for the reason given there and made explicit by Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-leak} below. The probe $J$ and the Hamiltonian $H$ are sparse in this basis, with known matrix elements $J_{yx}=\langle y|J|x\rangle$ and $H_{yx}=\langle y|H|x\rangle$.

The moment $\langle 0_\mathcal{S}|O_k|0_\mathcal{S}\rangle$ admits two evaluations, which coincide only when $\mathcal{S}$ is complete for the operators involved. Inserting the \emph{subspace} resolution $\Pi_\mathcal{S}$ in place of the identity yields the reconstruction (spectral) moment,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\bar m_k&=\sum_{n}(E_n^{\mathcal{S}}-e_0)^k|\langle n_{\mathcal{S}}|J|0_\mathcal{S}\rangle|^2\\
&=\langle 0_\mathcal{S}|J^\dagger\,[\Pi_\mathcal{S}(H-e_0)\Pi_\mathcal{S}]^{k}\,J|0_\mathcal{S}\rangle\\
&=\langle 0_\mathcal{S}|J_\mathcal{S}^\dagger\,(H_\mathcal{S}-e_0)^{k}\,J_\mathcal{S}|0_\mathcal{S}\rangle,\qquad J_\mathcal{S}=\Pi_\mathcal{S}J\Pi_\mathcal{S},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:app-recon}
\end{equation}
with the sum over the eigenpairs of $H_\mathcal{S}$. It is exactly $\int\omega^k A(\omega)\,d\omega$ and lives entirely inside $\mathcal{S}$. Here $H_\mathcal{S}-e_0$ acts on $\operatorname{span}\mathcal{S}$, so the $k=0$ term carries the convention $[\Pi_\mathcal{S}(H-e_0)\Pi_\mathcal{S}]^{0}\equiv(H_\mathcal{S}-e_0)^0\equiv\Pi_\mathcal{S}$, and ${\bar m_0=\langle 0_\mathcal{S}|J^\dagger\Pi_\mathcal{S}J|0_\mathcal{S}\rangle}$ is the weight captured by $\mathcal{S}$ rather than the full $\langle J^\dagger J\rangle$. The reconstruction-independent estimator instead applies the sparse operator $O_k=J^\dagger(H-e_0)^kJ$ directly,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\widehat{m}_k&=\langle 0_\mathcal{S}|J^\dagger(H-e_0)^kJ|0_\mathcal{S}\rangle\\
&=\sum_{x,x'\in\mathcal{S}}c_x^{*}c_{x'}\,\langle x|\,J^\dagger(H-e_0)^kJ\,|x'\rangle,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:app-indep}
\end{equation}
where each matrix element $\langle x|O_k|x'\rangle$ is a finite product of sparse operators summing over intermediate determinants that need \emph{not} lie in $\mathcal{S}$. This is the operational meaning of independence: $\widehat{m}_k$ probes the action of $J$ and $H$ on $|0_\mathcal{S}\rangle$ beyond the sampled subspace, whereas $\bar m_k$ is confined to it. Evaluated on $|0_\mathcal{S}\rangle$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-indep} is the shared-state estimator of Fig.~\ref{fig:teethshared}. Evaluated instead on the exact ground state $|0\rangle$ (with $e_0\to E_0$), it gives the exact moment $m_k$, where $O_k$ and $C_k$ coincide; this idealized version, more independent than a device allows, is used in the idealized curve of Fig.~\ref{fig:teethshared}, in the sealed blinded test (Sec.~\ref{sec:blind}, with Gaussian shot noise added), and in the Monte-Carlo and interval-battery demonstrations below.


\emph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:leak}.} Equation~\eqref{eq:app-leak} follows directly from Eqs.~\eqref{eq:app-recon} and~\eqref{eq:app-indep}, because both branches are built on the same probe state $\varphi_\mathcal{S}\equiv J|0_\mathcal{S}\rangle$ and the same reference $e_0$, and $J_\mathcal S|0_\mathcal S\rangle=\Pi_\mathcal S\varphi_\mathcal S$. (i) At $k=0$, with the convention above, it reduces to ${\langle\varphi_\mathcal{S}|(\openone-\Pi_\mathcal{S})|\varphi_\mathcal{S}\rangle}$\allowbreak${{}=\widehat m_0(1-w)\ge0}$, with ${w=\|\Pi_\mathcal{S}\varphi_\mathcal{S}\|^2/\|\varphi_\mathcal{S}\|^2}$ the companion's captured weight ($\Pi_\mathcal{S}$ is its $P_\mathcal{S}$)~\cite{SQDspec2608}. (ii) If $\operatorname{span}\mathcal{S}$ contains $\operatorname{span}\{\varphi_\mathcal{S},H\varphi_\mathcal{S},\dots,H^K\varphi_\mathcal{S}\}$, then ${[\Pi_\mathcal{S}(H-e_0)\Pi_\mathcal{S}]^j\varphi_\mathcal{S}=(H-e_0)^j\varphi_\mathcal{S}}$ for $j\le K$; writing $k=a+b$ with $a,b\le K$ for $k\le2K$, and $k=2K+1$ as $\langle(H-e_0)^K\varphi_\mathcal S|(H-e_0)|(H-e_0)^K\varphi_\mathcal S\rangle$ with $(H-e_0)^K\varphi_\mathcal S\in\operatorname{span}\mathcal S$, both terms of Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-leak} agree for every $k\le 2K+1$. This moment exactness is classical~\cite{Lanczos1950,GM2010}. (iii) If $w=1$, then $\Pi_\mathcal S\varphi_\mathcal S=\varphi_\mathcal S$, the $k=1$ terms agree, and at $k=2$ the difference is $\|(H-e_0)\varphi_\mathcal S\|^2-\|\Pi_\mathcal S(H-e_0)\varphi_\mathcal S\|^2=\|(\openone-\Pi_\mathcal{S})H\varphi_\mathcal{S}\|^2$, the companion's second-moment deficit. $\square$

Statement~(ii) explains why the under-converged Krylov reconstructions of Sec.~\ref{sec:blind} pass the low-order battery: an $n_l$-step Lanczos reconstruction started from the probe state projects onto its Krylov space with $K=n_l-1$ (the argument holds for any orthogonal projector, not only a determinant subspace) and so reproduces $m_0,\dots,m_{2n_l-1}$. The one-step case reproduces only $m_0$ and $m_1$; its $m_2$ is low by $m_0\operatorname{Var}_A$, $2$--$4\%$ on the catalog spectra, and passes below $\tau_2$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:blind}). Under determinant-subspace truncation the Krylov space is not contained, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-leak} measures the spectral weight the truncation leaks outside $\mathcal{S}$. Evaluated instead on the exact probe state $\varphi_0\equiv J|0\rangle$ (for the device, $J=c^\dagger_{0\uparrow}$), with only $\mathcal{S}$ taken from the device, the $k=0$ expression is the coverage residual of Sec.~\ref{sec:device}.

The full-support reconstruction of Fig.~\ref{fig:momentmc} is an instance: with $\mathcal{S}$ the whole $186$-determinant support of the $L=6$, $U/t=4$ ground state (so that $|0_\mathcal{S}\rangle=|0\rangle$ and $\varphi_\mathcal{S}=\varphi_0$), $w=1$, $\Delta_0$ and $\Delta_1$ vanish to machine precision, and $\Delta_2=0.62$ ($1.9\%$ of ${m_2=33.39}$); it captures all of $\varphi_\mathcal{S}$ and is still not exact. Against the default thresholds this reconstruction passes the full battery ($\Delta_0$ and $\Delta_1$ below $10^{-14}$ of $m_0$ and $m_1$, $\Delta_2=0.34\,\tau_2$; its leak score $\Delta_2/\widehat m_2=0.019$ flags it as not closed), although its relative $L_1$ error at $\eta=0.15\,t$ is $0.93$: Rayleigh--Ritz moves the dominant pole from $7.83\,t$ to $7.45\,t$ and the pole at $2.73\,t$ to $4.43\,t$. The same holds at $U/t=3$, $5$, $6$ and $8$, with $\Delta_2=0.20$--$0.36\,\tau_2$ and relative $L_1$ errors $0.68$--$0.99$ (\texttt{src/\allowbreak full\_support\_rr.py}, \texttt{data/\allowbreak 2026-10-02\_full\_support\_rr.json}). The companion also bounds the reconstruction error by $1-w$ from below and by the boundary coupling from above and shows that captured weight alone cannot certify; the use of these identities as a thresholded rejection test is this paper's (Sec.~\ref{sec:intro}). For the shared-state estimator, $\Delta_k=|\widehat{m}_k-\bar m_k|$ is nonzero only if $\mathcal{S}$ misses part of $\varphi_\mathcal{S}$ or of the states $H^j\varphi_\mathcal{S}$ that order $k$ probes; the independent estimator can then reject, while the circular control ($\bar m_k$ against itself) returns $\Delta_k\equiv 0$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:teeth}). Weight moved \emph{within} $\mathcal{S}$ contributes no leak term; it is visible only through the shift of the reconstruction moments that it causes, which Theorem~\ref{thm:detect} and Appendix~\ref{app:withinsector} characterize.

Equation~\eqref{eq:app-indep} is equivalently an importance-weighted average over $\mathcal{S}$,
\begin{equation}
\widehat{m}_k=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{S}}|c_x|^2\,O_k^{\mathrm{loc}}(x),\qquad O_k^{\mathrm{loc}}(x)=\frac{\langle x|O_k|0_\mathcal{S}\rangle}{\langle x|0_\mathcal{S}\rangle},
\end{equation}
the local-operator estimator familiar from variational Monte Carlo~\cite{CarleoTroyer2017}; the local moment $O_k^{\mathrm{loc}}(x)$ again sums over determinants connected to $x$ by $O_k$ and so reaches outside $\mathcal{S}$. Drawing $N_s$ configurations from $|c_x|^2$ and averaging $O_k^{\mathrm{loc}}$ gives an unbiased Monte-Carlo estimate with variance $\operatorname{Var}(O_k)/N_s$, where $\operatorname{Var}(O_k)=\sum_x|c_x|^2|O_k^{\mathrm{loc}}(x)|^2-\widehat m_k^2$ is the local-estimator variance. This form needs the amplitudes $c_x$, which a device does not return, so we use it only in simulation (Fig.~\ref{fig:momentmc} and the joint-sampling study below). On a device the same expectation would be estimated from Pauli-grouped measurements (Appendix~\ref{app:native}), with a variance of its own.

\emph{Same-sample statistics.} Strict same-shot estimation of both branches has been realized only in simulation, in a study sealed before it was run (SHA-256 \texttt{8bf87b5a\ldots}, 2026-09-01, self-attested; \texttt{data/\allowbreak 2026-09-01\_lever1\_prereg\_seal.json}). For the current probe of the $L=6$, $U/t=4$ ring it drew $2000$ faithful and $2000$ corrupted replicas at five coverages of the ground-state support, $84$, $90$, $95$, $99$ and $100\%$, reached with $2000$, $3200$, $5000$, $12000$ and $40000$ shots per replica; the sealed corruptions are $m_0$-preserving reweightings of each replica's reconstruction within its support (a tilt and a satellite, of both signs, at seven target shifts of $m_1$ from $2\%$ to $24\%$). The run applied one sign only, and its realized mean shifts of $\Delta_1$ are $0.4$--$20\%$ of $m_1$, not the targets. One set of shots per replica gives both $\widehat m_k$ and $\bar m_k$; here $\widehat m_k$ is the local estimator above, which uses the exact ground-state amplitudes, so it is not device-realizable. The sealed primary endpoints were the realized false-positive rate at the nominal $z$ and the detection-power difference at matched false-positive rate. The variance of the residual is $\operatorname{Var}(\Delta_k)=\operatorname{Var}(\widehat m_k)+\operatorname{Var}(\bar m_k)-2\operatorname{Cov}(\widehat m_k,\bar m_k)$, with a same-sample correlation coefficient of about $0.43$ at $84\%$ coverage, that is at $2000$ shots per replica rather than the $5\times10^4$ of the default rule; the correlation decays towards zero as coverage approaches $100\%$. Treating the two estimates as independent inputs, as one would when a moment interval comes from separate data, for example from the finite-statistics certificate of Mortimer \emph{et al.}~\cite{Mortimer2026}, over-estimates this variance, and the test becomes over-conservative in the undersampled regime (realized false-positive rate $1.5\%$ at a nominal $5\%$). At matched false-positive rate the correlation-aware test gives no detection-power advantage (power differences between $-5.6$ and $+0.05$ percentage points across the ladder of corruptions and coverages that was run), so the covariance is a calibration correction, not a power gain. That comparison used the covariance estimated from the Monte Carlo itself, an oracle, and gives point estimates without confidence intervals (\texttt{data/\allowbreak 2026-09-01\_lever1\_oracle.json}); no single-sample plug-in stage was run, since the sealed decision gate calls for one only if the oracle stage shows a gain. This structure is a quantum instance of Hausman-type specification testing~\cite{Hausman1978}: $\widehat m_k$ is the consistent, model-free estimator and $\bar m_k$ the model-implied one. The independent-input variance $\operatorname{Var}(\widehat m_k)+\operatorname{Var}(\bar m_k)$ is exact only at full coverage, where the correlation vanishes; Hausman's efficiency limit, $\operatorname{Cov}(\widehat m_k,\bar m_k)=\operatorname{Var}(\bar m_k)$, would instead reduce the residual variance to $\operatorname{Var}(\widehat m_k)-\operatorname{Var}(\bar m_k)$, and our correlation lies between the two limits.

The exact-moment test takes its finite-shot form here. We model the estimator uncertainty as $\delta_k^2=\operatorname{Var}(O_k)/N_s+b_k^2$, a shot term over $N_s$ samples plus a bias budget $b_k$ for mitigation and device error, and reject the reconstruction at level $\alpha$ when
\[
\Delta_k=|\widehat{m}_k-\bar m_k|>\tau_k,\qquad \tau_k\equiv z_{1-\alpha/2}\,\delta_k .
\]
The default rule (used here only in simulation and on classically computed moments) takes $\alpha=0.05$ per moment ($z_{0.975}=1.96$), $N_s=5\times10^4$ and $b_k=0.02\,|\widehat m_k|$; for the three-moment battery the sealed nominal joint level is ${1-0.95^3=14.3\%}$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:blind}). For the current probe of the doped $L=6$, $U/t=4$ ring, with $\operatorname{Var}(O_k)$ and $\widehat m_k$ taken on the exact ground state (\texttt{data/\allowbreak 2026-09-27\_interval\_battery.json}), it gives $\tau_0=0.029$, $\tau_1=0.229$ and $\tau_2=1.848$. The level holds only while the estimator's bias stays within $b_k$, which is assumed, not measured: a rejection shows an inconsistency between estimate and reconstruction, and attributing it to the reconstruction rests on that assumption. A second, separate check concerns the estimate alone: whether some moment vector in the box $[\widehat m_\ell-\tau_\ell,\widehat m_\ell+\tau_\ell]$ satisfies the positivity conditions obeyed by the moments of every positive measure, $H_n\succeq0$ and, for one-sided $T=0$ support, $H_n^{(1)}\succeq0$. Infeasibility would show that the estimated moments belong to no spectrum, pointing to the device, the mitigation or the estimator; it cannot refute a non-negative reconstruction, whose own moments $\bar m_k$ satisfy these conditions by construction. In the implementations reported here (the sealed blinded test and the interval battery below) the positivity condition is evaluated at $\bar m_k$ and therefore cannot reject; every rejection comes from a residual $\Delta_k$. The operators, targets and intervals of each demonstration are in the reproduction materials.

We close with a Monte-Carlo calibration of this test (Fig.~\ref{fig:momentmc}) at $N_s=5\times10^4$, the per-circuit budget of the sealed rule (that of the companion's \texttt{ibm\_fez} run, and the largest of our later $L=8$ budgets). It is a simulation: $M=4\times10^3$ replicas of $N_s$ shots are drawn from the exact ground-state distribution $|\langle x|0\rangle|^2$ of the doped $L=6$, $U/t=4$ ring, a system not run on hardware; $\widehat m_1$ is the local estimator above for its current probe, and each truncated reconstruction is built from the ground state of the truncated Hamiltonian. The Monte Carlo checks the analytic interval rather than replacing it. The realized estimator is close to Gaussian (sample skewness $-0.04$ and excess kurtosis $-0.05$ over the replicas, consistent within sampling error with the exact $-0.011$ and $3.5\times10^{-4}$ of the $5\times10^4$-shot mean), its spread matches the analytic shot term to $1\%$ ($0.0799$ against $0.0806$), and its false-positive rate, $4.5\%$ (Wilson $95\%$ interval $[3.9,5.2]\%$), is consistent with the nominal $5\%$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:momentmc}a). Two inputs are modeled: the shot term uses the exact-state local-estimator variance, which assumes known amplitudes and omits ratio-estimator and Pauli-grouping overhead, so it likely understates the per-shot uncertainty of a device estimator; the bias term $b_k$ is assumed and swept, not measured. For this probe $\operatorname{Var}(O_1)\approx3.2\times10^{2}$ gives a shot standard error $\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(O_1)/N_s}\approx0.081$ ($1.9\%$ of $m_1$); with the $2\%$ bias budget $\tau_1=0.229$ ($5.4\%$ of $m_1$), close to the $5\%$ relative line of Fig.~\ref{fig:teethshared}.

Power is high but not uniform in the truncation depth. At the shot-only threshold $m_1$ alone reaches power $\ge0.95$ at $161$ of the $185$ truncations, already at $d=181$ ($97\%$ coverage, power $0.98$). The exceptions are the four mildest truncations ($d=182$--$185$, one to four determinants dropped) and three narrow windows, $d=84$--$88$, $93$--$98$ and $158$--$166$ (coverage $45$--$47\%$, $50$--$53\%$, $85$--$89\%$), at whose centers power falls to the false-positive level ($5.5\%$, $6.1\%$, $5.0\%$ at $d=86$, $95$, $162$). The $22$-point grid of Fig.~\ref{fig:momentmc}b touches the first two windows only at their edges ($d=88$, $98$; power $0.93$, $0.94$) and misses the third. The noise-free residuals agree: at the default $2\%$ bias budget $|\widehat m_1-\bar m_1|<\tau_1$ for $17$ of the $185$ truncations ($d=183$--$185$, $159$--$165$, $94$--$97$, $85$--$87$), and at a $4\%$ budget for $27$.

Where the cut falls matters. The probabilities $|\psi_0|^2$ of this sector come in exactly degenerate shells of $6$, $12$ or $24$ determinants, and which members of a shell a truncation keeps is a convention. All the window cuts, and $19$ of the $21$ truncated grid points, fall inside a shell. Our convention is deterministic (descending $|\psi_0|^2$, ties broken by sector index), but verdicts near the windows depend on it. At $d=98$ it gives $|\widehat m_1-\bar m_1|=0.284>\tau_1$, while $300$ random choices of the $8$ kept members of the $24$-fold shell give $0.172$--$0.295$ and rejection by $m_1$ alone in $57\%$ of cases. At the ten cuts that fall on a shell boundary ($d=6,18,30,42,66,90,114,138,150,174$), and so do not depend on the convention, the noise-free residual of $m_1$ alone exceeds $\tau_1$ at the $2\%$ budget.

Detection sets in at much higher coverage here than in Fig.~\ref{fig:teethshared}, whose idealized full-state and shared-state curves cross their $5\%$ line near $7\%$ and $18\%$ retained determinants ($d\approx1.6$--$4.4\times10^4$ of $2.4\times10^5$; Fig.~\ref{fig:teethshared}). This reflects a different test, not a sharper one: those curves score the large $L=12$ sector by a \emph{relative} discrepancy $|\widehat m_1-\bar m_1|/\widehat m_1$ against a fixed line, this Monte Carlo the small $L=6$ sector by an \emph{absolute} threshold set by the shot budget. The coverage percentages thus come from different rulers on different-sized sectors (and, at $L=12$, two reference states), not from one test giving several answers.

The direction of the residual uncertainty matters for a test that can only reject. Underestimating $\delta_k$ shrinks $\tau_k$: safe against missed errors (false negatives, acceptable for a necessary condition) but anti-conservative against false rejections (false positives, the costly error for such a test). The false-positive rate above relies on $\bar m_1$ being exact at full support ($|\widehat m_1-\bar m_1|\sim10^{-15}$), so that the null hypothesis holds exactly there. For $m_2$ it does not: the $186$-determinant support contains all of $\varphi_\mathcal{S}$ but not $H\varphi_\mathcal{S}$, so the reconstruction on it is wrong at second order, $\Delta_2=0.62$ ($0.48\,\tau_2$ at the shot-only threshold, $0.34\,\tau_2$ at the default one), the boundary-leakage term above. An $m_2$ rejection of it would be a correct detection, not a false positive; the Monte Carlo tests $m_1$ only. Away from full coverage the threshold should be set on $\operatorname{Var}(\widehat m_k-\bar m_k)$, for which $\operatorname{Var}(O_k)$ is a proxy valid only when the reference is trusted (same-sample statistics above; Sec.~\ref{sec:limits}).

The second moment catches some lone-$m_1$ misses, not all. In the noise-free scan at the $2\%$ budget, $m_2$ rejects $3$ of the $17$ truncations that $m_1$ alone passes ($d=97$, $96$, $85$), and $m_0$ rejects two more ($d=94$ and $159$, residuals $0.044$ and $0.037$ against $\tau_0=0.029$). The clearest is $d=97$ ($52\%$ coverage): in the deterministic order $|\widehat m_1-\bar m_1|=0.186<\tau_1=0.229$ but $|\widehat m_2-\bar m_2|=2.73>\tau_2=1.85$, whatever the tie convention ($m_1$ alone rejects in none and the battery in all of $300$ random shell choices). With exact moments the full $(m_0,m_1,m_2)$ battery nonetheless passes the remaining $12$ truncated reconstructions at the $2\%$ budget: $d=183$--$185$ ($98$--$99.5\%$ coverage), $160$--$165$ ($86$--$89\%$), and $86$, $87$, $95$ ($46$--$51\%$). Some of these passes are tie-convention effects (the battery rejects in $44\%$, $40\%$ and $11\%$ of random shell choices at $d=87$, $160$ and $95$), but not those at $d=86$ ($1.3\%$), $161$--$165$ (at most $0.3\%$) or $183$--$185$ (none). Without a bias budget the battery catches the mid-coverage windows but still passes $d=161$--$164$, $184$ and $185$; with a $4\%$ budget it passes $22$ truncations. The joint battery is thus a partial remedy for the single-moment windows: with finite thresholds no battery of necessary conditions is free of blind spots, and at the default order this one has blind spots that we can list. The shared-state estimator passes a different set, $16$ near-complete truncations (Sec.~\ref{sec:rr}).

In this demonstration the discrepancy is measured against the exact moments, which serve as an oracle: it calibrates the interval where classical ground truth exists, and a device-side version that propagates measured rather than assumed bias remains to be done.

\section{Blinded test: protocol details}
\label{app:blinddetails}
\emph{Decision rule.} The sealed rule rejects when any $|\widehat m_k-\bar m_k|>\tau_k$, $k\le2$ (the thresholds of Sec.~\ref{sec:estimator}, $z=1.96$), when the interval-Hankel condition on the estimates fails, or when the $m_1$ interval lies entirely below zero (tolerance $10^{-6}$). The implementation evaluates $\operatorname{Var}(O_k)$ with $H$ shifted by the exact $E_0$ (Appendix~\ref{app:estimator}).

\emph{Deviations from the sealed protocol.} Three departures of the implementation from the sealed text, and one post hoc analysis, qualify the result. (i) The sealed catalog calls the spurious feature a Gaussian atom and gives no width; the generator adds a point atom. (ii) The sealed rule applies the interval-Hankel condition and the $m_1$-sign clause to the estimates. The classifier evaluated both on the reconstruction moments, as positivity of the $2\times2$ Hankel matrix $H_1$ of $\bar m_0,\bar m_1,\bar m_2$ and as $\bar m_1\ge0$, which cannot fail on a catalog of positive measures on $\omega\ge0$ (smallest eigenvalue of $H_1$ over the $300$ instances $-3.6\times10^{-15}$, rounding error). That branch rejected $0/300$ by construction: every rejection came from the moment comparisons, and the sealed battery acted as the three comparisons alone. (iii) The sealed shot model sets the noise standard deviation to $z$ times the local-estimator standard error; the generator draws it with the standard error itself, $\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(O_k)/N_s}$, the shot term inside $\tau_k$; the reported $1/112$ rests on this scale, and the rescoring at the sealed scale ($\widehat m_k-m_k\to1.96\,(\widehat m_k-m_k)$, threshold unchanged) is the right-hand column of Table~\ref{tab:blind}. (iv) The shared-state rescoring of Sec.~\ref{sec:blind} was not pre-registered and covers the $56$ truncations. The sealed files were not modified, and the reported endpoint is the sealed one.

\emph{Shared-state rescoring.} The rebuild of the sealed truncations depends on the stored eigensolver tie order inside degenerate $|\psi_0|^2$ shells. The rescoring uses $\widehat m_k=\langle0_\mathcal{S}|J^\dagger(H-e_0)^kJ|0_\mathcal{S}\rangle$ plus the sealed noise draw and variances; before noise, $\widehat m_0-\bar m_0=\widehat m_0(1-w)$ is exactly the weight of $J|0_\mathcal{S}\rangle$ outside $\mathcal{S}$, and the $m_0$ comparison alone rejects all $56$. The $m_1,m_2$ thresholds reuse the sealed variances, which are centered on $E_0$ rather than $e_0$; this leaves the $m_0$ verdicts unchanged. The oracle-centered sealed estimator rejected on $m_0$ in $55$ of the $56$; the exception, id~$162$ ($r_0=0.62$, rejected only off-diagonally), is an artifact of the oracle-centered estimate and has $r_0=15.8$ under the shared-state estimator; its shared-state estimate $\widehat m_0=1.04$ is far from the exact $m_0=0.37$.

\emph{Bookkeeping of Fig.~\ref{fig:separating}.} The $26$ instances with $r_0<1$ but an off-diagonal residual above threshold are the $20$ $m_0$-preserving spurious features, $4$ weight-changing artifacts whose weight shift fell below $\tau_0$, truncation id~$162$ and false positive id~$184$. The other $14$ preserving and $23$ changing artifacts form the legend entry ``other spurious'' ($37$). Reading the $20/34$ $m_0$-preserving catches as a capability is post hoc; low detection was pre-registered. Theorem~\ref{thm:detect} does not apply to these artifacts: they add a pole, leaving its fixed-pole class.

\section{Illustrations: single-particle and collective channels}
\label{app:illustrations}
The figures of this appendix apply the construction to three more probes with injected corruptions; no threshold is calibrated for them.

Figure~\ref{fig:akw} shows $A(k,\omega)$ of the half-filled Hubbard ring, a one-dimensional correlated insulator with Hubbard bands separated by the Mott gap~\cite{Hubbard1963,LiebWu1968,Dagotto1994}. The single-particle gap approaches $U$ only in the atomic limit $t\to0$, and at the size ($L=12$) and broadening of the figure the spinon and holon branches are only partly resolved. An injected corruption flattens the band-centroid dispersion by moving weight between the Hubbard bands at each momentum, leaving the Mott gap and the per-$k$ total weight intact. For this probe $m_0$ is pinned to $1$, so the first informative moment is $m_1$~\cite{Lehmann1954,Freericks2009}: from the anticommutator sum rule for the non-Hermitian probe $c_{k\sigma}$, measured from $U/2$, it is the Hartree centroid $\epsilon_k+U(\langle n_{-\sigma}\rangle-\tfrac12)=\epsilon_k$ at half filling (Appendix~\ref{app:moments}), and it exposes the flattening (right panel). The corruption moves the centroid by a fixed fraction of $\epsilon_k$ and the moment is closed-form, so the residual is set by construction and no threshold is calibrated for this probe: the panel illustrates the check rather than testing it.

The moment vector of every positive measure lies in the moment body: its Hankel matrices, and its localizing matrices on any interval known to contain the support, are positive semidefinite~\cite{ShohatTamarkin1943,Akhiezer1965}. A violated Hankel condition on the estimated moments rejects the estimate without reference to $A(\omega)$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:momentcone}); it cannot reject a non-negative reconstruction. In the blinded test this branch was applied to the reconstruction moments, where it cannot fail, and rejected none of the $300$ instances; it has not been applied to estimated moments anywhere in this paper.

Wang \emph{et al.}\ turn the same machinery into certified two-sided bounds on many-body ground-state properties~\cite{WangAcin2024}, and Mortimer \emph{et al.}\ extend it to finite measurement statistics~\cite{Mortimer2026}. Applied to the operators $O_k$ on the ground state, these semidefinite programs (SDPs) would bound the true moments, $m_k\in[\ell_k,u_k]$; this is possible in principle but not done in the cited works or here. Membership of the read-off moments $\bar m_k$ in such intervals would be a second necessary condition, of the same logical type as ours; their conjunction is at least as strict as either alone and is not a composition with a sufficient condition.

The classical moment-body conditions (not the Wang/Mortimer programs) illustrate such bounds at exact moments on the $L=6$, $U/t=8$ current measure of Appendix~\ref{app:proof} (outside the blinded catalog's $U/t\in\{3,4,5,6\}$), normalized to $m_0=1$ (true $m_2=125.4$). Positivity gives $m_2\ge m_1^2/m_0=123.3$. For a support contained in $[a,b]$, the Hausdorff condition ${\int(\omega-a)(b-\omega)\,d\mu\ge0}$ gives $m_2\le(a{+}b)m_1-ab\,m_0$: $147.6$ for the weighted support $[2.49,13.92]\,t$, which is oracle knowledge requiring the exact spectrum; a~priori, $243.3$ for the excitation range $[0,21.91]\,t$ of the probe's four-electron, $S_z=0$ sector and $579.8$ for the full Fock range $[0,52.21]\,t$ ($573.7$ with the lower edge at the smallest positive Fock excitation, $0.148\,t$). An under-dispersed moment vector ($m_2=122.1$) violates the positivity bound; an over-dispersed one ($m_2=148.8$) violates only the oracle Hausdorff bound and passes both a~priori bounds. Neither condition has been applied to device-estimated moments.

The two layers could meet on integrated window weight: over all positive measures that share given moments, the window mass $\mu([c,d])=\int_c^d A\,d\omega$ ranges over an interval, given in closed form at exact moments by Proposition~\ref{prop:markovkrein} (the Markov--Krein envelope, of width at most $W_n(c)+W_n(d)$), and an SDP fed with interval-valued moments~\cite{Mortimer2026} would give a finite-statistics counterpart against which a reconstruction's window mass could be checked. We do not implement this. Nor do we attempt frequency-resolved weights, a limitation rather than a stylistic choice: a weight $|\langle n|J|0\rangle|^2$ at a resolved frequency is an excited-state matrix element, outside the ground-state moment hierarchy that makes these SDPs tractable; the resolved line shape almost certainly remains beyond this machinery.

\section{The Christoffel bracket and the window bound: statements, proof and evaluation}
\label{app:proof}
\newtheorem{applemma}{Lemma}% own counter: Lemmas 1--3 of this appendix (cited by number in Proposition~2)
This appendix states what a \emph{passing} test implies for cumulative and window weight. The bound below is classical (moments and orthogonal polynomials); our contribution is its evaluation as a diagnostic for a reconstructed spectrum, not the mathematics, and the Christoffel--Darboux kernel is an established data-analysis tool~\cite{GM2010,Szego1975,PauwelsLasserre2019,LasserrePauwelsPutinar2022}. Let $F(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}A(\omega)\,d\omega$ be the cumulative weight of a diagonal $T=0$ Lehmann measure whose moments $m_0,\dots,m_{2n}$ are known exactly. Here $x$ is a frequency; Fig.~\ref{fig:christoffel} plots it after an affine map onto $[-1,1]$, which does not change $W_n$ (Appendix~\ref{app:evaluate}), and the hopping $t$ remains the energy unit. The moments fix the orthonormal polynomials $p_0,\dots,p_n$ via the Hankel system~\cite{ShohatTamarkin1943}, and the Christoffel function is $W_n(x)\equiv\lambda_n(x)=1/K_n(x,x)=1/\sum_{j=0}^{n}p_j(x)^2$, with $K_n(x,y)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}p_j(x)p_j(y)$ the Christoffel--Darboux kernel (construction from the moments in Appendix~\ref{app:evaluate})~\cite{Szego1975,GM2010}.

\begin{theorem}[Cumulative-weight miss-distance bound; classical, re-derived]
\label{thm:christoffel}
Let $A(\omega)$ be a diagonal $T=0$ Lehmann spectrum, so that ${d\mu=A(\omega)\,d\omega}$ is a positive measure; let it have compact support and a positive-definite Hankel matrix $H_n=[m_{i+j}]_{i,j=0}^{n}$, and suppose the moments $m_0,\dots,m_{2n}$ are known exactly. For $x\in\mathbb{R}$ define the cumulative-weight miss-distance as the extremal spread $\sup|F_{\nu_1}(x)-F_{\nu_2}(x)|$ over all positive measures $\nu_1,\nu_2$ on $\mathbb{R}$ sharing those moments. Then for every $x\in\mathbb{R}$ this spread equals the Christoffel function $W_n(x)$, which is also the supremum of the mass a moment-matching measure can place at $x$ (the Gauss--Radau weight $\lambda_n(x)$, attained except at the $n$ zeros of the orthonormal polynomial $p_n$). In particular, any non-negative reconstruction (a positive measure, as every Lehmann reconstruction is) whose moments $m_0,\dots,m_{2n}$ equal the true ones obeys $|F_{\mathrm{rec}}(x)-F_{\mathrm{true}}(x)|\le W_n(x)$ at every $x\in\mathbb{R}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:proof})~\cite{KarlinStudden1966,DetteStudden1997}.
\end{theorem}

\noindent\emph{Remarks.} No convex-hull hypothesis is needed: for $H_n\succ0$ the Gauss--Radau rule with a node at $x$ has positive weights for every real $x$ except the $n$ zeros of $p_n$, and its atom at $x$ is $W_n(x)$ (Appendix~\ref{app:proof}). The bound is monotone, $W_{n+1}\le W_n$. For $N$ distinct poles, $H_n\succ0$ only for $n\le N-1$, and at a pole $W_n(\omega_i)\ge w_i>0$ (the residue), with equality at $n=N-1$: $W_n$ does not vanish on the support. In the thermodynamic limit, where the discrete spectrum densifies, $W_n(x)\to\mu(\{x\})$, which vanishes away from atoms for any compactly supported measure~\cite{ShohatTamarkin1943,Nevai1986}; the $1/n$ rate, $n\,W_n(x)\to A(x)/\omega_S(x)$, is established for Stahl--Totik regular measures obeying a local Szeg\H{o} condition (Appendix~\ref{app:proof})~\cite{Totik2000,MNT1991}.

The equality is the classical content of the Chebyshev--Markov--Stieltjes inequalities and Gauss--Christoffel moment theory~\cite{GM2010,ShohatTamarkin1943,Akhiezer1965}. We say ``bound'', not ``certify'': the test never certifies that $A(\omega)$ is correct. The bounded object is cumulative weight, not the pointwise value of $A(\omega)$ or a peak position~\cite{GM2010}, and the contraction \emph{rate} is established under that regularity~\cite{Totik2000,MNT1991}. The top panel of Fig.~\ref{fig:christoffel} shows the mechanism: the true $F(x)$ lies between the extremal Gauss--Radau envelopes, a bracket of width $W_n(x)$.

The theorem assumes exact moments; the default test passes a reconstruction whose residuals lie within tolerances, $|\Delta_k|\le\tau_k$, and only through order $2n=2$. We evaluate the consequences on the measure of Fig.~\ref{fig:christoffel}, the doped $L=6$, $U/t=8$ current measure. It lies outside the blinded catalog ($U/t\in\{3,4,5,6\}$, Sec.~\ref{sec:blind}) and is not the $U/t=4$ measure of Appendix~\ref{app:withinsector}; we use it as an illustrative measure (the $U/t=4$ check below gives the same conclusion), and the thresholds used below are those the default rule would give on this state. There are two consequences. First, at $n=1$ the exact-moment bracket is the one-sided Chebyshev (Cantelli) bound~\cite{KarlinStudden1966}, $W_1(\omega)=m_0\,\sigma^2/[\sigma^2+(\omega-\bar\omega)^2]$ with mean $\bar\omega=11.10\,t$ and variance $\sigma^2=2.10\,t^2$; its maximum $m_0$ lies at the mean, $0.01\,t$ from the dominant atom ($11.09\,t$), so the bracket is vacuous at the peak, and just above the top pole, at $\omega=14\,t$, it is still $0.20$. Second, tolerances loosen it further: the largest atom that a positive measure with $|m_k-m_k^{\mathrm{true}}|\le\tau_k$ ($k\le2$, the default threshold rule evaluated on this state) can place at $\omega$ is $3.2$--$7.7$ times $W_1$ at $\omega=14$--$20\,t$ ($0.63$ against $W_1=0.20$ at $14\,t$), $10$--$11$ times $W_1$ far outside the band ($\omega\approx34$--$50\,t$), and more than $m_0$ at the mean ($1.045\,m_0$). On the $U/t=4$ measure of Appendix~\ref{app:withinsector} the same computation gives a similar inflation, $3.6$--$5.7$ times $W_1$ at $\omega=12$--$20\,t$, above its top pole at $10.66\,t$. At exact-moment orders $n\le4$ ($m_0,\dots,m_8$) the global maximum falls only to $\max_xW_4=0.882$, and at $n=N-1=4$, $W_4$ at the dominant atom equals its residue $0.778$, below which no order can go (panel~c; Appendix~\ref{app:evaluate}). No positive-order moment beyond $m_2$ is estimated in this work. At the default order, passing the test therefore places no constraint on the cumulative weight at the peak. The companion shows by example that exact moments through order $2K+1$ coexist with an $O(1)$ reconstruction error~\cite{SQDspec2608}; the Christoffel function makes this distribution-free at the default order, for every measure that passes the test.

Refutation and bracketing are separate statements. Rejection ($|\Delta_k|>\tau_k$ for some $k$) does not involve $W_n$, and its reach is set by the thresholds (Sec.~\ref{sec:detect}); a Hankel/Stieltjes violation of the \emph{estimated} moments rejects the estimate, not a non-negative reconstruction. $W_n$ governs only how tightly a \emph{passing} reconstruction's cumulative weight is bracketed. The looseness above therefore limits what passing implies; it does not limit the power to reject.

At a frequency the measure leaves empty, however, $W_n(x)$ bounds the weight any moment-matching reconstruction can place there; far above the band ($\omega\approx34$--$50\,t$), $W_1=1.4$--$4\times10^{-3}$ and the higher orders are far smaller (Appendix~\ref{app:evaluate}). These small values reflect the distance from the band, not resolution: at $n=1$ they are the Cantelli tail. Nearer the band the bracket is wider ($W_1=0.20$ at $\omega=14\,t$), and the default tolerances raise the far-out-of-band $n=1$ values about tenfold. This is a computed property of this measure, not a theorem, and we do not extrapolate it to the two-sided single-particle Mott gap, a distinct measure not tested here.

\begin{proposition}[Two-sided Markov--Krein window-mass bound]
\label{prop:markovkrein}
Let $d\mu=A(\omega)\,d\omega$ be a positive measure (a diagonal $T=0$ Lehmann spectrum) with $H_n=[m_{i+j}]_{i,j=0}^{n}\succ0$, and suppose $m_0,\dots,m_{2n}$ are known exactly. For real $x$ with $p_n(x)\neq0$, let $F_-(x)$ be the mass that the $(n{+}1)$-point Gauss--Radau rule with a node fixed at $x$ places strictly left of $x$, and let $F_+(x)=F_-(x)+W_n(x)$, where $\lambda_n(x)=W_n(x)$ is that rule's atom at $x$; at the $n$ zeros of $p_n$, where the rule does not exist, both are taken as limits. These are the lower and upper ends of the Chebyshev--Markov--Stieltjes bracket on $F(x)$ (the ordinate-gap Lemma~\ref{lem:gap} of Appendix~\ref{app:proof}). For any frequency window $[c,d]$ with $c\le d$ real, the mass $\mu([c,d])=\int_c^d A(\omega)\,d\omega$ of \emph{every} positive measure sharing those moments obeys
\begin{equation}
\max\{0,\,F_-(d)-F_+(c)\}\ \le\ \mu([c,d])\ \le\ F_+(d)-F_-(c)\ \le\ m_0 .
\label{eq:mk-window}
\end{equation}
The bracket is two-sided; its upper end never exceeds $m_0$, since $F_+(d)\le m_0$ and $F_-(c)\ge0$. Before the lower end is clipped at $0$ its width is exactly $W_n(c)+W_n(d)$; after it, the width is still at least $\max\{W_n(c),W_n(d)\}$, because $F_\pm$ are non-decreasing. The bracket is therefore tight exactly when $W_n$ is small at both window edges, and it inherits the monotone contraction $W_{n+1}\le W_n$. The sharp bounds, the infimum and supremum of $\mu([c,d])$ over all positive measures sharing $m_0,\dots,m_{2n}$ (the Markov--Krein extremal problem~\cite{KreinNudelman1977}), are no wider. Equation~\eqref{eq:mk-window} follows directly from the Chebyshev--Markov--Stieltjes inequalities and extends the point bound on the cumulative weight, $|F_{\mathrm{rec}}-F_{\mathrm{true}}|\le W_n$, to a two-sided bound on integrated weight~\cite{KreinNudelman1977,KarlinStudden1966,DetteStudden1997,GM2010}.
\end{proposition}

Evaluated on the same $L=6$, $U/t=8$ current measure, again at exact moments (Appendix~\ref{app:evaluate}, evaluation of the Markov--Krein window bound), the bracket~\eqref{eq:mk-window} is narrow at $n=4$ for a window whose two edges both lie where this measure has no weight: a peak-containing window gives $\mu\in[0.9732,0.9758]$ around the exact $0.9744$; at the default order $n=1$ the same window gives the wider $[0.848,1]$. It is as loose as $W_n$ when an edge sits on the peak, and the default tolerances loosen it further. The narrowness at weight-free edges is a computed property of this measure, on which $W_n$ is small at those edges; it is not part of Proposition~\ref{prop:markovkrein}. In general $W_n$ need not be small where a measure has no weight: $W_1=m_0$ at the spectral mean, which for two equal atoms is the empty midpoint between them. Optimal moment bounds on a broadened line shape go back to Gordon~\cite{Gordon1968}; the bound here is also the real-axis, ground-state-moment counterpart of the Euclidean-time two-sided spectral bounds obtained from the moment problem analytically, via Kovalishina's solution~\cite{AbbottJayOare2025}, or by semidefinite programming~\cite{CausalBootstrap2026,CertifiedSpectral2026}; its moments are ground-state operator expectations, not lattice correlators. As with the one-sided bound, we do not extrapolate it to the two-sided single-particle Mott gap.

\subsection*{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:christoffel}}
We re-derive the equality of the extremal cumulative-weight spread with the Christoffel function from three classical lemmas~\cite{Akhiezer1965,KreinNudelman1977,GM2010}; nothing in this derivation is new. Here $x$ is a real frequency point in any affine frame (Fig.~\ref{fig:christoffel} uses the rescaled frame $x\in[-1,1]$); the hopping $t$ remains the energy unit. Write $\mathcal{P}_n$ for the real polynomials of degree at most $n$, $K_n(x,y)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}p_j(x)p_j(y)$ for the Christoffel--Darboux kernel and $\lambda_n(x)=1/K_n(x,x)$ for the Christoffel function, and assume the moment vector is interior to the moment cone, $H_n=[m_{i+j}]_{i,j=0}^{n}\succ 0$ (for a finite $N$-atom Lehmann spectrum this holds for $n\le N-1$). The measures $\nu$ compared below are positive measures on $\mathbb{R}$. Restricting them to the Lehmann half-line $\omega\ge0$ can only narrow the bracket, so $W_n$ remains an upper bound there.

\begin{applemma}[Variational identity]\label{lem:variational}
$\lambda_n(x)=\min\{\int|P|^2\,d\mu : P\in\mathcal{P}_n,\ P(x)=1\}$, with minimizer $P^\star=K_n(\cdot,x)/K_n(x,x)$.
\end{applemma}
\begin{proof}
Expanding $P=\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_jp_j$ gives $\int|P|^2\,d\mu=\sum_j c_j^2$, while by Cauchy--Schwarz
\[
1=P(x)^2=\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_jp_j(x)\Big)^2\le\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_j^2\Big)K_n(x,x),
\]
with equality at $c_j=p_j(x)/K_n(x,x)$; the minimum is $1/K_n(x,x)=\lambda_n(x)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{applemma}[Maximal atom]\label{lem:atom}
Among positive measures $\nu$ with $\int\omega^k\,d\nu=m_k$ for $k\le 2n$, the supremum of the mass at a point $x$ is $\sup_\nu\nu(\{x\})=\lambda_n(x)$. It is attained if ${p_n(x)\neq0}$, and approached but not attained at the $n$ zeros of $p_n$.
\end{applemma}
\begin{proof}
The minimizer $P^\star$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:variational} has $P^\star(x)=1$, so
\[
\nu(\{x\})=P^\star(x)^2\,\nu(\{x\})\le\int (P^\star)^2\,d\nu=\int (P^\star)^2\,d\mu=\lambda_n(x),
\]
the middle equality using $\deg (P^\star)^2=2n$ and that $\nu$ shares those moments. If ${p_n(x)\neq0}$ the bound is attained by the $(n{+}1)$-point Gauss--Radau rule with a node prescribed at $x$: a positive measure matching $m_0,\dots,m_{2n}$ whose weight at $x$ is $\lambda_n(x)$. At the $n$ zeros of $p_n$ this rule does not exist (one node escapes to infinity), and the supremum is approached but not attained.
\end{proof}

\begin{applemma}[Ordinate gap]\label{lem:gap}
Let ${p_n(x)\neq0}$, and let $\xi_0<\dots<\xi_n$ be the nodes of the Gauss--Radau rule of Lemma~\ref{lem:atom}, one of them equal to $x$, with weights $\lambda_n(\xi_i)>0$. For every positive $\nu$ sharing $m_0,\dots,m_{2n}$, with $F_\nu(x)=\nu((-\infty,x])$, the Chebyshev--Markov--Stieltjes inequalities~\cite{Akhiezer1965,KreinNudelman1977,KarlinStudden1966,DetteStudden1997} hold:
\[
\textstyle\sum_{\xi_i<x}\lambda_n(\xi_i)\ \le\ F_\nu(x^-)\ \le\ F_\nu(x)\ \le\ \sum_{\xi_i\le x}\lambda_n(\xi_i).
\]
Consequently $\sup_{\nu_1,\nu_2}|F_{\nu_1}(x)-F_{\nu_2}(x)|=\lambda_n(x)$, and the same supremum holds at the zeros of $p_n$, so it holds for every real $x$.
\end{applemma}
\begin{proof}
The inequalities follow from polynomials $Q_\pm\in\mathcal{P}_{2n}$ with $Q_-\le\mathbf{1}_{(-\infty,x)}$ and $\mathbf{1}_{(-\infty,x]}\le Q_+$ that coincide with these indicators at the nodes, so that $\int Q_\pm\,d\nu=\int Q_\pm\,d\mu$ equal the two sums. The bracket has width $\lambda_n(x)$. Its upper end is attained by the Radau rule itself; its lower end is approached by Radau rules whose prescribed node $x'$ decreases to $x$. At the zeros of $p_n$ the same supremum follows from the continuity of $\lambda_n$ and the right-continuity of $F$.
\end{proof}

Combining the three lemmas, the extremal spread equals $\lambda_n(x)=W_n(x)$ for every real $x$, and any single reconstruction consistent with the moments obeys $|F_{\mathrm{rec}}(x)-F_{\mathrm{true}}(x)|\le W_n(x)$. No convex-hull hypothesis is needed: the Radau rule has positive weights for every $x$ with ${p_n(x)\neq0}$, inside or outside the convex hull of the support. We checked this on the $U/t=8$ current measure of Fig.~\ref{fig:christoffel}: $24$ Radau rules ($n=1,\dots,4$ at six values of $x$, $20$ of them outside the convex hull of the weighted support) all have positive weights, place mass $W_n(x)$ at $x$ to a relative error of $2\times10^{-16}$, and match $m_0,\dots,m_{2n}$ to $1.4\times10^{-17}$. Monotonicity $W_{n+1}\le W_n$ follows from $K_{n+1}=K_n+p_{n+1}^2\ge K_n$.

For any compactly supported measure, $W_n(x)\to\mu(\{x\})$ as $n\to\infty$~\cite{ShohatTamarkin1943,Nevai1986}, so in the thermodynamic limit, where the discrete spectrum densifies, $W_n$ vanishes away from atoms; regularity is needed only for the rate. If the discrete spectrum densifies to a measure that is Stahl--Totik regular with compact support $S$ and absolutely continuous with a density $A$ obeying the local Szeg\H{o} condition $\int_I\log A>-\infty$ on an interval $I\subset S$, then $n\,W_n(x)\to A(x)/\omega_S(x)$ for almost every $x\in I$, with $\omega_S$ the equilibrium density of $S$~\cite{Totik2000}; on a single interval this reduces to M\'at\'e--Nevai--Totik~\cite{MNT1991}.

\noindent\emph{Evaluation of the bound from the moments.}\label{app:evaluate}\enspace
The Christoffel function is computed from the moments $\{m_k\}_{k=0}^{2n}$ by a short pipeline~\cite{GolubWelsch1969,GM2010}. With the inner product $\langle f,g\rangle_\mu=\int f(\omega)g(\omega)\,d\mu(\omega)$, so that $\langle\omega^a,\omega^b\rangle_\mu=m_{a+b}$, Gram--Schmidt on $\{1,\omega,\dots,\omega^n\}$ gives the orthonormal polynomials, with $p_0=1/\sqrt{m_0}$; they obey the three-term recurrence
\begin{equation}
\beta_{j+1}\,p_{j+1}(\omega)=(\omega-\alpha_j)\,p_j(\omega)-\beta_j\,p_{j-1}(\omega),
\label{eq:app-recur}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha_j=\langle\omega p_j,p_j\rangle_\mu$ and $\beta_j=\langle\omega p_j,p_{j-1}\rangle_\mu$, all fixed by $\{m_k\}$. The map from power moments to $(\alpha_j,\beta_j)$ becomes ill-conditioned as $n$ grows, so high orders need high-precision arithmetic. The nodes and weights of the $(n{+}1)$-point Gauss rule are the eigenpairs of the Jacobi matrix $\mathbf{J}_{n+1}=\operatorname{tridiag}(\beta_j,\alpha_j,\beta_j)$ (Golub--Welsch), and $K_n(x,x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}p_j(x)^2$, evaluated by the same recurrence, gives $W_n(x)=1/K_n(x,x)$. Every quantity in the bound is thus an explicit function of the moments, readable at any frequency.

We evaluate it on the $L=6$, $U/t=8$ current measure of Fig.~\ref{fig:christoffel}, normalized to $m_0=1$; five of its atoms carry weight. (Its ground level is a spin triplet; the current measure is the same for all three states, because $J$ is a spin scalar.) This measure lies outside the blinded catalog ($U/t\in\{3,4,5,6\}$, Sec.~\ref{sec:blind}) and differs from the $U/t=4$ spectrum of Appendix~\ref{app:withinsector}; we use it as an illustrative measure, and the in-catalog $U/t=4$ measure gives the same tolerance inflation (below). The numbers below come from \texttt{src/\allowbreak export\_christoffel\_dat.py} and \texttt{src/\allowbreak christoffel\_tolerance\_lp.py} (\texttt{data/\allowbreak 2026-09-27\_theory\_numerics.json}). We run the recurrence in $60$-digit arithmetic on the atoms and weights of the measure, which fix the same coefficients as its moments, and locate the maxima by root-finding: $\max_xW_n=1,\ 0.969,\ 0.886,\ 0.882$ for $n=1,\dots,4$. The $n=1$ maximum equals $m_0$ and lies at the mean frequency $\bar\omega=11.10\,t$, the zero of $p_1$, so at the default order ($m_0,m_1,m_2$) the bracket is vacuous at the spectral peak. At the dominant atom ($\omega=11.09\,t$, residue $0.778$), $W_n$ reaches the residue at $n=N-1=4$, the highest order at which $H_n$ is positive definite, and for $n\le4$ it cannot go lower, because Lemma~\ref{lem:atom} with $\nu=\mu$ gives $W_n(x)\ge\mu(\{x\})$: within the range of the theorem no number of moments narrows the bracket at the peak below $0.778$. For $n>4$, $H_n$ is singular and the theorem's hypothesis fails.

The rescaled abscissa $x\in[-1,1]$ of Fig.~\ref{fig:christoffel} is the affine image of the frequency range $\omega\in[0.148,\,52.21]\,t$, the positive excitation energies of the full Fock space, zero-weight states included. The weighted support $[2.49,\,13.92]\,t$ maps to $[-0.910,\,-0.471]$, and $73.5\%$ of $[-1,1]$ lies above the last weighted pole. The affine map moves points along the axis but does not change $W_n$, which is a mass. The rescaled points $x=0.3$, $0.6$, $0.9$ correspond to $\omega=34.0$, $41.8$, $49.6\,t$, far above the band. There $W_1=4.0\times10^{-3}$, $2.2\times10^{-3}$, $1.4\times10^{-3}$, and at $x=0.6$ the higher orders give $W_2=1.4\times10^{-5}$, $W_3=2.7\times10^{-8}$, $W_4=3.6\times10^{-10}$. For $n=1$ these small values are Cantelli's one-sided Chebyshev bound, $W_1(\omega)=m_0\sigma^2/[\sigma^2+(\omega-\bar\omega)^2]$ with $\sigma^2$ the variance of the normalized measure (it agrees with the orthogonal-polynomial evaluation to $1.6\times10^{-15}$). They reflect the distance from the mean, not a feature of this spectrum. Just above the top pole, at $\omega=14\,t$, $W_1=0.20$.

All of this assumes exact moments. If the moments are matched only within the default tolerances, $|\Delta_k|\le\tau_k$ for $k\le2$, the largest atom a positive measure can place at $\omega$ is a linear program. With thresholds from the default rule applied to this state ($\tau_0=0.051$, $\tau_1=0.57$, $\tau_2=6.5$ before normalization; an illustration, since the rule was calibrated only on the catalog), it is $3.2\,W_1$ ($0.63$ of $m_0$) at $\omega=14\,t$, $7.7\,W_1$ at $20\,t$, and $10.1$--$10.7\,W_1$ at the rescaled points $x=0.3$--$0.9$. On the $U/t=4$ measure of Appendix~\ref{app:withinsector}, which is in the catalog, the same construction gives $3.6$--$5.7\,W_1$ at $\omega=12$--$20\,t$, so the loosening is not specific to $U/t=8$. These $\tau_k$ use the exact-state local-estimator shot variance of Appendix~\ref{app:estimator}, which is expected to underestimate a realistic per-shot variance; wider tolerances would inflate the atom further. At the default order a passing reconstruction is therefore bracketed much more loosely than $W_1$ alone suggests.

\emph{Evaluation of the Markov--Krein window bound (Proposition~\ref{prop:markovkrein}).} On the same $L=6$, $U/t=8$ current measure (committed script \texttt{src/\allowbreak markov\_krein\_window.py}), at exact moments: at $n=4$ ($m_0,\dots,m_8$), a window with one edge at the $400$-point-grid maximizer of $W_4$, just above the dominant atom, gives $\mu\in[0.070,0.945]$, width $0.875$; the exact maximum of $W_4$, at a nearby frequency, is $0.882$. A window that contains the peak with both edges in weight-free frequency (rescaled $[-0.75,-0.35]$) gives $\mu\in[0.9732,0.9758]$ around the exact $0.9744$, and the empty window rescaled $[0.3,0.9]$ ($\omega\approx34$--$50\,t$) gives $\mu\in[0,3.2\times10^{-9}]$. The peak window is tight here because $W_4$ is small at both of its edges (before the lower end is clipped at $0$ the width is $W_4(c)+W_4(d)$); weight-free edges alone do not ensure this, as $W_1=0.20$ at $14\,t$, just above the band, shows, so this is a property of this measure. At the default order $n=1$ ($m_0,m_1,m_2$) the peak-containing window gives $\mu\in[0.848,1]$ and the empty window $\mu\in[0,0.004]$, while a window with an edge on the peak is uninformative (its bracket runs from $0$ to within $2\times10^{-4}$ of $m_0$). These are exact-moment brackets. Under the default tolerances the upper end for the empty window is at least $0.040$, the largest atom that a measure with $m_0,m_1,m_2$ within tolerance can place at its lower edge ($\omega\approx34\,t$).

\section{Within-sector redistributions and the default battery}
\label{app:withinsector}
This appendix proves Theorem~\ref{thm:detect} and evaluates it on one spectrum. The independent estimator detects weight that leaks \emph{outside} the retained determinant support. A redistribution strictly \emph{within} it contributes no leak term; it is visible only through the moment shift $(V\bm{\delta})_k$, and not at all if it preserves the moments used (Sec.~\ref{sec:teeth}). We treat that residual in three steps: \emph{injectivity} (part~(a): in exact arithmetic, $m_0,\dots,m_{N-1}$ fix the weights on $N$ distinct poles); \emph{conditioning} (part~(b) and the Corollary: a sufficient and a necessary condition for exposing a redistribution at finite shots); and \emph{reach} (a linear program and explicit counterexamples on the doped $L=6$, $U/t=4$ current spectrum, with thresholds from Appendix~\ref{app:estimator}; Table~\ref{tab:withinsector}). The result at the default order is negative: the battery $(m_0,m_1,m_2)$ has no guaranteed power against within-sector redistributions of this spectrum, although its poles are well separated. We characterize this residual; we do not close it. As in Theorem~\ref{thm:detect}, $K$ denotes the highest order a redistribution preserves; the default battery tests orders $0$, $1$ and $2$. Unless stated otherwise, the numbers below are in \texttt{data/\allowbreak 2026-09-27\_theory\_numerics.json} (script \texttt{src/\allowbreak within\_sector\_lp.py}).

\emph{Injectivity (proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:detect}(a)).} A $T=0$ Lehmann current spectrum on a finite sector is a discrete measure on $N$ distinct poles $\{\omega_n\}$,
\[
A(\omega)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}w_n\,\delta(\omega-\omega_n),\qquad w_n=\sum_{a:\,E_a-E_0=\omega_n}|\langle a|J|0\rangle|^2\ge0,
\]
where $w_n$ is the total weight of the eigenstates at $\omega_n$. Its moments are $m_k=\sum_n w_n\,\omega_n^{k}$, i.e.\ $\mathbf{m}=V\mathbf{w}$ with $V_{kn}=\omega_n^{k}$ a Vandermonde matrix. Because the poles are distinct, the block $V_{0:N-1}$ of rows $0,\dots,N-1$ is invertible, so the weight-to-moment map is injective through order $N-1$: a redistribution $\mathbf{w}\to\mathbf{w}+\bm{\delta}$ with $\bm{\delta}\neq0$ cannot preserve all of $m_0,\dots,m_{N-1}$. A redistribution that preserves $m_0,\dots,m_K$ ($K<N-1$) zeroes the first $K{+}1$ entries of $V_{0:N-1}\bm{\delta}$, so $\|(\Delta_{K+1},\dots,\Delta_{N-1})\|_2=\|V_{0:N-1}\bm{\delta}\|_2\ge\sigma_{\min}(V_{0:N-1})\|\bm{\delta}\|_2$; the largest of these $N{-}1{-}K$ shifts is at least this norm divided by $\sqrt{N-1-K}$, which is Eq.~\eqref{eq:detect-sigmamin}. If the poles may also move, an $N$-atom measure has $2N$ parameters and is fixed by $m_0,\dots,m_{2N-1}$ among measures with at most $N$ atoms (Prony), and by $m_0,\dots,m_{2N}$ among all positive measures, since its Hankel matrix $H_N$ is singular~\cite{ShohatTamarkin1943,PottsTasche2013}. Weight traded among degenerate eigenstates at a single frequency changes no moment and leaves $A(\omega)$ unchanged; it is not an error.

For the $L=6$, $U/t=4$ spectrum the sixteen eigenstates with nonzero weight fall on $N=5$ distinct frequencies, $\omega_n/t=2.73$, $5.57$, $7.83$, $8.70$, $10.66$, with weights $0.040$, $0.004$, $0.393$, $0.116$, $0.001$ ($m_0=0.554$; the pole at $7.83\,t$ carries $71\%$ of it). The map is therefore injective through $m_4$: a redistribution among these poles that preserves $m_0,\dots,m_K$ changes some $m_j$ with $K<j\le4$ (at most $m_9$ if the poles also move and the altered spectrum has at most five poles; $m_{10}$ against any positive measure). In exact arithmetic the residual is thus resolved by $m_4$. That is two orders beyond the default battery, and it says nothing about detection at finite shots. Equation~\eqref{eq:detect-sigmamin} is valid here but loose: for the redistribution CE1 below, its right-hand side is $3.8\times10^{-3}$, while the larger of the shifts in $m_3$ and $m_4$ is $85$. The sufficient condition of Theorem~\ref{thm:detect}(b)(i) is far from met here: for CE1 and CE2 the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:detect-sigmamin} is below $4\times10^{-3}$, against $\max_j\tau_j=\tau_4=122$ in the same units.

\emph{Conditioning, and when detection is possible (proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:detect}(b)).} Invertibility of $V_{0:N-1}$ holds in exact arithmetic. At finite shots a shift counts only if it exceeds the threshold $\tau_j$. Two bounds, in opposite directions, turn detectability into a statement about the size of $\bm{\delta}$ and the geometry of the poles it touches.

\emph{Sufficient condition.} By Eq.~\eqref{eq:detect-sigmamin}, a redistribution preserving $m_0,\dots,m_K$ is exposed (its noise-free shift exceeds the threshold) at some order $j\le N-1$ if
\[
\frac{\sigma_{\min}(V_{0:N-1})\,\|\bm{\delta}\|_2}{\sqrt{N-1-K}}>\max_{K<j\le N-1}\tau_j .
\]
Since $\|\bm{\delta}\|_1/\sqrt{N}\le\|\bm{\delta}\|_2\le\|\bm{\delta}\|_1$, this is a condition on $\rho=\|\bm{\delta}\|_1$ up to a factor~$\sqrt{N}$. It is informative only when $\sigma_{\min}$ is not small, and it uses moments up to order $N-1$.

\emph{Necessary condition.} Let $s\ge2$, and let $\bm{\delta}$ preserve $m_0,\dots,m_{s-2}$ and be supported on poles inside an interval of diameter $D$ and midpoint $\omega_c$. (Among $s$ poles, preserving $m_0,\dots,m_{s-2}$ leaves a one-parameter family, and $m_{s-1}$ is the first moment it changes.) Since $\sum_n\delta_n\,q(\omega_n)=0$ for every polynomial $q$ of degree at most $s-2$, the signed shift is $\sum_n\delta_n\,\omega_n^{s-1}=\sum_n\delta_n\,q(\omega_n)$ for every monic $q$ of degree $s-1$. The choice $q(\omega)=2^{2-s}(D/2)^{s-1}\,T_{s-1}\big(2(\omega-\omega_c)/D\big)$, the monic Chebyshev polynomial of the interval, has modulus at most $2(D/4)^{s-1}$ on it, hence
\[
|\Delta_{s-1}|\le2\rho\,(D/4)^{s-1}.
\]
The bound is sharp: weights of alternating sign on the $s$ Chebyshev extrema of the interval, with the discrete orthogonality weights that preserve $m_0,\dots,m_{s-2}$, attain it. The cruder choice $q=(\omega-\omega_c)^{s-1}$ gives $\rho\,(D/2)^{s-1}$, larger by $2^{s-2}$.
Subtracting from $\omega^j$ its Taylor polynomial of degree $s-2$ at $\omega_c$ gives, for every ${j\ge s-1}$, the Lagrange-remainder bound
\[
|\Delta_j|\le\rho\binom{j}{s-1}\big(|\omega_c|+D/2\big)^{j-s+1}(D/2)^{s-1};
\]
expanding instead $\omega^{j}=\sum_i\binom{j}{i}\omega_c^{\,j-i}(\omega-\omega_c)^i$, whose terms with ${i\le s-2}$ are combinations of preserved moments and vanish, and bounding the rest by $|\omega_n-\omega_c|\le D/2$ gives the term-by-term form of \mbox{Theorem}~\ref{thm:detect}(b)(ii). Either way every shift vanishes as $D^{s-1}$. Exposure at order $s-1$ therefore requires $2\rho\,(D/4)^{s-1}>\tau_{s-1}$. With $\tau_{s-1}=z_{1-\alpha/2}\,[\operatorname{Var}(O_{s-1})/N_s+b_{s-1}^2]^{1/2}$ this reads
\[
N_s>\frac{z_{1-\alpha/2}^{2}\operatorname{Var}(O_{s-1})}{4\rho^2(D/4)^{2(s-1)}-z_{1-\alpha/2}^2\,b_{s-1}^2},
\]
and no budget exposes it at order $s-1$ once $2\rho\,(D/4)^{s-1}\le z_{1-\alpha/2}\,b_{s-1}$. The shot cost of detecting a redistribution of fixed size inside a collapsing cluster thus grows at least as $D^{-2(s-1)}$. Numerically, the identity holds to a relative error of $1.5\times10^{-12}$, and the bound holds in $11$ structured cases (CE1 and CE2 below, the six optima of Eq.~\eqref{eq:wclp} and three committed random cases) and in $20\,000$ random null-space redistributions on random pole subsets (seed $20260927$, no violation). The largest ratio $|\Delta_{s-1}|/[2\rho(D/4)^{s-1}]$ is $1.000$, $0.998$ and $0.924$ at $s=2,3,4$ (against the cruder $\rho(D/2)^{s-1}$: $1.000$, $0.499$ and $0.231$), the sharp form also holds in every case, and alternating weights on the Chebyshev extrema attain it to a ratio of $1.000000$. If a cluster contracts uniformly, so that its minimal pole separation falls from $\Delta\omega_{\min}$ to $\Delta\omega$, $D$ shrinks by the same factor and the lower bound~\eqref{eq:detect-Ns} on the shot cost grows by at least $(\Delta\omega_{\min}/\Delta\omega)^{2(s-1)}$. A finite separation below which no budget suffices exists only with a bias floor, $b_{s-1}>0$.

\emph{Conditioning numbers.} The same collapse appears in $\sigma_{\min}$: for $s$ nodes of fixed relative geometry and diameter $D\to0$, $\sigma_{\min}$ of the $s\times s$ Vandermonde block scales as $D^{s-1}$. The sharp super-resolution results for this regime~\cite{BatenkovClustered2020,KunisNagel2021,Donoho1992}, like the separation threshold of Ref.~\cite{Moitra2015}, are proved for Fourier (unit-circle) nodes; for real nodes and power moments, worst-case error bounds of matching order for a near-colliding cluster are given in Ref.~\cite{AkinshinGoldmanYomdin2021}. For the fixed-pole case needed here both directions follow from elementary bounds on the $s\times s$ block $V$ of the cluster. Row $n$ of $V^{-1}$ holds the coefficients of the Lagrange polynomial $\prod_{j\ne n}(\omega-\omega_j)/(\omega_n-\omega_j)$, which gives Gautschi's bound~\cite{Gautschi1962,Gautschi1963} and hence
\[
\|V^{-1}\|_\infty\le\max_n\prod_{j\ne n}\frac{1+|\omega_j|}{|\omega_n-\omega_j|},\qquad
\sigma_{\min}(V)\ge\Big[\sqrt{s}\,\max_n\prod_{j\ne n}\frac{1+|\omega_j|}{|\omega_n-\omega_j|}\Big]^{-1},
\]
a lower bound that scales as $D^{s-1}$ at fixed relative geometry. Conversely, the redistribution that spans the null space of rows $0,\dots,s-2$ has $\|V\bm{\delta}\|_2=|\Delta_{s-1}|\le\rho\,(D/2)^{s-1}\le\sqrt{s}\,\|\bm{\delta}\|_2\,(D/2)^{s-1}$, so $\sigma_{\min}(V)\le\sqrt{s}\,(D/2)^{s-1}$. A direct check with $s=2,3,4$ equally spaced nodes near~${\omega=7.83\,t}$ (spacing $0.02$--$0.2\,t$) gives fitted exponents $0.995$, $1.98$ and $2.96$ (\texttt{data/\allowbreak 2026-08-24\_within\_sector\_control.json}). Condition numbers depend on the frequency unit, so we quote them only on nodes mapped affinely onto $[-1,1]$ (outermost poles at $\pm1$): for the five poles above, $\sigma_{\min}(V_{0:4})=0.056$ and $\kappa=50$. With a minimal separation of $0.87\,t$ these poles are well separated, yet good conditioning does not give the default battery guaranteed power: that is set by the dimension of a null space (next paragraph).

\emph{Reach of the default battery (instantiation of Theorem~\ref{thm:detect}(b) and Corollary).} The zero-power clause of part~(b) is immediate: a vector in the null space of rows $0,\dots,K{+}1$ of $V$, scaled so that $w_n+\delta_n\ge0$ (possible because every $w_n>0$), preserves $m_0,\dots,m_{K+1}$; every smaller rescaling does too, so the smallest shift at order $K{+}1$ is zero. The default battery uses $m_0,m_1,m_2$ with the thresholds of Appendix~\ref{app:estimator}, $\tau_0=0.0287$, $\tau_1=0.229$ and $\tau_2=1.848$, at $N_s=5\times10^4$ with an assumed, unmeasured $2\%$ bias budget $b_k=0.02\,m_k$; each $\tau_k$ includes the factor $z_{0.975}=1.96$ and is not a $1\sigma$ noise floor. The shot term uses the exact-state local-estimator variance, which is expected to underestimate the true per-shot variance, so realistic thresholds would be wider. For a redistribution that preserves $m_0,\dots,m_K$, the largest shift it can impose on the next moment is the linear program
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\Delta_{K+1}^{\max}=\max_{\bm{\delta}}\ \Big|\textstyle\sum_n \delta_n\,\omega_n^{K+1}\Big|\quad\text{s.t.}\\[2pt]
\textstyle\sum_n\delta_n\,\omega_n^{j}=0\ (j\le K),\ \ \delta_n\ge -w_n,
\end{split}
\label{eq:wclp}
\end{equation}
the constraint $\delta_n\ge-w_n$ keeping the corrupted weights non-negative; together with $\sum_n\delta_n=0$ it also bounds $\|\bm{\delta}\|_1\le2m_0$. Extremal problems of this kind over the moment space are classical~\cite{KarlinStudden1966,KreinNudelman1977}; here they are evaluated on one measured spectrum.

Table~\ref{tab:withinsector} gives the full range of the signed shift. A redistribution that preserves $m_0,m_1$ can move $m_2$ by up to $7.09=3.8\,\tau_2$, so some such redistributions are exposed at $m_2$. The guaranteed power, however, is set by the smallest shift a redistribution of a given size can produce, and that is zero. Rows $0,1,2$ of $V$ have a null space of dimension $N-3=2$: some redistributions preserve $m_0$, $m_1$ and $m_2$ exactly, and scaling one of them down gives an exactly invisible redistribution of every smaller size. Three explicit cases, all positive measures that pass the default battery: (i)~CE1 preserves $m_0,m_1,m_2$ exactly and moves $72\%$ of the weight ($\|\bm{\delta}\|_1/2=0.72\,m_0$); it empties two of the five poles and lowers the dominant residue from $0.393$ to $0.037$. (ii)~CE2 preserves $m_0,m_1$ exactly and shifts $m_2$ by exactly $\tau_2$, so it sits on the decision boundary and passes only because the rule rejects on the strict inequality $\Delta_k>\tau_k$; it moves $86\%$ of the weight and lowers the dominant residue to $0.073$. (iii)~CE3 keeps $|\Delta_k|\le\tau_k$ for $k\le2$, has $\|\bm{\delta}\|_1=2.03\,m_0$ (above $2m_0$ because $m_0$ itself moves by $\tau_0$), and empties three poles, the dominant one included. The random $K=1$ case committed in \texttt{src/\allowbreak within\_sector\_control.py} (seed $0$) moves $25\%$ of the weight ($\|\bm{\delta}\|_1/2=0.25\,m_0$; its output file lists $\|\bm{\delta}\|_1/m_0=0.49$ as the moved fraction) and shifts $m_2$ by $0.044\,\tau_2$; it passes as well. The guaranteed power of the default battery against within-sector redistributions of this spectrum is therefore zero. The reason is the dimension of the null space, not conditioning, and the same holds for any spectrum with four or more distinct poles.

Adding $m_3$ and $m_4$ would remove the null space (rows $0,\dots,4$ have $\dim\ker=0$), but at this shot budget the extended battery would still miss a redistribution that moves $72\%$ of the weight. Under the same threshold rule, $\tau_3=14.98$ and $\tau_4=122.0$. Among redistributions that preserve $m_0,m_1,m_2$ exactly, the largest $m_3$ shift is $5.26=0.35\,\tau_3$ (Table~\ref{tab:withinsector}), and CE1, like every scaled-down copy of it, stays below both thresholds ($0.23\,\tau_3$ and $0.70\,\tau_4$). Some such redistributions would be caught at $m_4$: the one that maximizes the $m_3$ shift moves $m_4$ by $145=1.19\,\tau_4$, a marginal case, since the variance behind $\tau_4$ is expected to underestimate a realistic one. CE2 and CE3, which use the tolerance on $m_2$, would be caught ($|\Delta_3|=3.3\,\tau_3$; $|\Delta_4|=7.1$ and $7.2\,\tau_4$).

The joint battery does catch some errors that $m_1$ alone misses, but the example is a truncation, not a fixed-pole redistribution: truncating the determinant basis of the same system to the $d=97$ determinants of largest $|\psi_0|^2$ ($52\%$ of the $186$-determinant support; Appendix~\ref{app:estimator}) gives a reconstruction that $m_1$ alone passes and $m_2$ rejects. The cut falls inside a $24$-fold degenerate shell, so the gaps depend on how ties are ordered. With the deterministic \texttt{lexsort} order used by both the smoke test \texttt{src/verify.py} and \texttt{src/\allowbreak interval\_battery.py} (descending $|\psi_0|^2$, ties by ascending determinant index), $|\bar m_1-\widehat m_1|=0.186<\tau_1$ and $|\bar m_2-\widehat m_2|=2.729>\tau_2$; and for all $300$ random tie-breaks within the shell $m_1$ alone rejects none and the battery rejects all (\texttt{data/\allowbreak 2026-09-27\_interval\_battery.json}). This is one catch, not a guarantee: in the exhaustive scan of Appendix~\ref{app:estimator} ($d=1,\dots,185$, same thresholds, \texttt{lexsort} order) $m_1$ alone passes $17$ truncated reconstructions, $m_2$ rejects three of them ($d=97$, $96$, $85$) and $m_0$ two more ($d=94$, $159$), so with exact moments the full $(m_0,m_1,m_2)$+Hankel battery passes $12$ ($d=86$, $87$, $95$, $160$--$165$ and $183$--$185$). Under random tie-breaks within the degenerate shells, ten of these pass in at least $89\%$ of $300$ draws, and $d=87$ and $d=160$ in $56\%$ and $60\%$.

Positivity cannot help against within-sector redistributions: a redistribution with non-negative weights is itself a positive measure, so the Hankel and Stieltjes conditions hold by construction. Only independent moments of high enough order, estimated precisely enough, can expose it, and at the default order the guaranteed power is zero. We report this residual; we do not claim to close it.

\section{Complementary state-level check: the Gauss law}
\label{app:gauss}
The Gauss-law check mentioned in Secs.~\ref{sec:estimator} and~\ref{sec:limits} is not a moment test; we record its construction and scope here. In the U(1) lattice Schwinger model the generators $G_x$ of gauge transformations commute with the Hamiltonian, $[H,G_x]=0$, and every physical state satisfies $G_x|\psi\rangle=0$ at every site, so $\langle\sum_x G_x^2\rangle=0$ exactly. The operator $\sum_x G_x^2$ is diagonal in the computational basis, so on hardware it could be estimated from computational-basis samples of the prepared state, with no reconstruction and no moment. It tests the prepared state, not a reconstructed spectrum: it responds to leakage out of the physical sector, an error class the moment test does not target.

Figure~\ref{fig:gauss} illustrates the identity on a small instance: the quantum-link form of the model with $N=4$ staggered matter sites and $3$ spin-$\tfrac12$ links, open boundaries, $w=1$, $m=0.6$ ($[H,G_x]=0$ to machine precision). The plotted state is $|\psi(\epsilon)\rangle\propto(1-\epsilon)|\psi_0\rangle+\epsilon|\phi_u\rangle$, with $|\psi_0\rangle$ the physical ground state and $|\phi_u\rangle$ an unphysical state. The parameter $\epsilon$ is an amplitude, not a weight: the unphysical weight is $f_u(\epsilon)=\epsilon^2/[(1-\epsilon)^2+\epsilon^2]$, $0.059$ at $\epsilon=0.2$ and $0.155$ at $\epsilon=0.3$. Because $\sum_xG_x^2|\psi_0\rangle=0$ and $\langle\psi_0|\phi_u\rangle=0$, the violation is $\langle\sum_xG_x^2\rangle=g_u\,f_u(\epsilon)$ with $g_u=\langle\phi_u|\sum_xG_x^2|\phi_u\rangle$. The curve is therefore fixed by construction: zero for the physical state, rising monotonically with $\epsilon$, with its scale set by $g_u$ alone. It illustrates the identity and does not measure detection power. The values are computed exactly on the state vector; no sampling is simulated.

The admixed state is chosen by an explicit rule: $|\phi_u\rangle$ is the lowest-energy eigenstate of $H$ among charge-neutral states with the smallest nonzero violation, $\sum_xG_x^2=2$ (one site with $G=+1$ and one with $G=-1$; a $27$-dimensional subspace; generator \texttt{src/\allowbreak export\_gauss\_state.py}). Every state in that subspace has $g_u=2$, so the plotted curve is $2f_u(\epsilon)$. The plotted values follow from this rule (\texttt{data/\allowbreak 2026-09-27\_gauss\_state.json}). Over the $124$ unphysical eigenstates of this model $g_u$ ranges from about $1$ to $10$, so the slope of the curve reflects this choice. Under an independent bit-flip channel on every qubit the violation is positive at every nonzero flip rate ($0.25$ at $p=0.05$; $40$ sampled flip patterns per rate, too few for a monotone curve: $3.05$ at $p=0.35$, $2.52$ at $p=0.40$; \texttt{data/\allowbreak 2026-08-18\_gausslaw\_falsifier.json}).

Gauss-law violation has been measured in a $71$-site cold-atom quantum simulator~\cite{Gauss2020} and analyzed theoretically under gauge-breaking errors~\cite{HalimehHauke2020}. The lattice Schwinger model is also the model of self-verifying variational simulation on a trapped-ion analog quantum simulator~\cite{Kokail2019} and of early digital simulations of Schwinger-model dynamics~\cite{Martinez2016,Klco2018}.

\section{Device run: reference samplers, line shapes and complete supports}
\label{app:device}
This appendix records the remaining reference comparisons and the line-shape comparisons of the $L=8$ device run (Sec.~\ref{sec:device}), and the complete-support runs at $L=6$. As in that section, $\Delta_0$ measures support coverage and rewards support spreading; it is not a fidelity diagnostic.

\emph{(iv) Noiseless sampling at the device's retained counts.} This comparison depends on how the retained counts are allocated. Matched circuit by circuit, the comparator puts $46{,}216$ of its $82{,}697$ shots at $50$k on the $t=0$ circuit, which is a single determinant without noise; its mean $\Delta_0$ is $0.224$, $0.279$, $0.343$ and $0.443$ at $50$/$30$/$16$/$4$k. With the same total split equally over the seven circuits it is $0.150$, $0.205$, $0.273$ and $0.404$. Under both allocations the device lies below every replica at $50$, $30$ and $16$k; at $4$k it lies at the $0.6$th (per circuit) or $5.8$th (equal split) percentile. The margin shrinks with the budget: the reference mean exceeds the device value $51$-, $8.6$-, $2.8$- and $1.19$-fold per circuit, and $34$-, $6.3$-, $2.2$- and $1.08$-fold with the equal split.

\emph{(ii) Uniform noise (details).} Mixing each circuit's output distribution $P_k$ with a fraction $p$ of uniformly random bitstrings, $(1-p)P_k+p/2^{16}$, before post-selection, changes the expected $\Delta_0$ without being monotone in $p$: at every budget the exact $\mathbb{E}[\Delta_0](p)$ falls from $p=0$ to a single minimum and rises again toward $p=1$, with the minimum at $p\approx0.93$, $0.88$, $0.77$ and $0.09$ at $50$/$30$/$16$/$4$k ($\mathbb{E}[\Delta_0]=0.0020$, $0.017$, $0.075$, $0.269$). At $50$k it falls from $0.036$ at $p=0$ to $0.0056$ at $p=0.5$ and is $0.0024$ at $p=1$. At $4$k it is flat to $0.3\%$ up to $p=0.25$ and then rises to $0.326$ at $p=1$, because post-selection keeps only $6\%$ of uniform shots. At the $p$ that reproduces the device's retention ($p\approx0.81$), the mixture's mean ($0.0023$, $0.017$, $0.075$, $0.295$) is below the device at every budget. The device nevertheless lies inside the mixture distribution at $50$--$16$k ($85.7$th, $91.6$th and $88.7$th percentiles of $1000$ replicas) and at its far upper edge only at $4$k ($99.9$th; one replica lies higher).

\emph{Line shape.} On the line shape (the relative $L_1$ error of Sec.~\ref{sec:device}), too, noiseless sampling (comparator (i)) does worse at the two higher budgets. Its reconstructions give $0.566\pm0.032$ at $50$k (all $80$ replicas above the device) and $0.630\pm0.043$ at $30$k (device at the $1.25$th percentile). At $16$k and $4$k the device is worse ($96$th percentile, and above all $80$ replicas). The uniform in-sector sampler gives $0.014$ at $16$k and $0.90$ at $4$k, below the device at every budget.

\emph{Complete supports.} The companion study ran its pipeline at $L=6$ (\texttt{ibm\_fez}, $12$ qubits, $3.5\times10^{5}$ shots ($5\times10^{4}$ on each of seven circuits; job \texttt{d9s16avpemts73ct6g8g}); $L=6$ Hubbard model, $U/t=4$, addition sector $4\!\uparrow,3\!\downarrow$ ($300$ configurations), $\eta=0.15\,t$ in its spectrum; measurement twirling, Pauli gate twirling and dynamical decoupling, no readout-error mitigation)~\cite{SQDspec2608}. Its retained support is the whole addition sector (${|\mathcal S|=300/300}$), so the reconstruction equals exact diagonalization by coverage. The bitstrings were post-selected after being read in reversed qubit order, which swaps the spin labels and moves the circuits' own output ($4\!\uparrow,3\!\downarrow$, read as $3\!\uparrow,4\!\downarrow$) into the discarded $S_z=-\tfrac12$ sector, so all $300$ retained determinants came from device errors~\cite{SQDspec2608}. Its raw counts are not deposited. The run shows only that the pipeline executes; no moment residual can be nonzero on a complete support. Two earlier $L=6$ \texttt{ibm\_fez} jobs of ours (2026-08-21, seven circuits of the same family at $U/t=4$ and $3.5$, $3.5\times10^{4}$ shots each; raw records deposited) likewise retain the whole $300$-configuration sector under either bit order, so a reconstruction on it is exact diagonalization of the assumed Hamiltonian, and a moment test on it returns the same verdict with or without the device data. They enter no result.

\section{Cost of a device-native estimator}
\label{app:native}
This appendix estimates the resources and the noise bias of measuring $m_1,m_2$ on hardware, and describes a diagonal alternative; none of it was run on a device.

The estimator also admits a device-native evaluation. We verify it in simulation because it settles how the off-diagonal (hopping) content of $\widehat m_1,\widehat m_2$ would be obtained on hardware. For a probe diagonal in the computational basis, the density probe $\rho_q=\sum_j\cos(qj)\,n_j$ at $q=\pi$, the moment operator is $O_k$ of Appendix~\ref{app:moments} with $J=\delta\rho_\pi\equiv\rho_\pi-\langle\rho_\pi\rangle$ and $e_0=E_0$. It is a sum of Pauli strings, and ${\widehat m_k=\sum_P c_P\langle P\rangle}$, the expectation $\langle 0|O_k|0\rangle$ written as a linear combination of Pauli expectations, is estimated from counts. The $m_0$ term is a pure computational-basis estimator. The hopping terms in $m_1,m_2$ need rotated-basis measurements, one circuit per qubit-wise-commuting group of Pauli strings, the standard grouping for Pauli observables; classical shadows are a randomized alternative~\cite{HuangKuengPreskill2020}. This path measures a prepared ground state in rotated bases, with circuits separate from those whose samples define $\mathcal{S}$: it shares the device with the reconstruction but not the samples.

We verify it by finite-shot sampling of the exact ground state of an open $L=6$ Hubbard chain at $U/t=4$ ($12$ qubits), with $5\times10^4$ shots in each of the $357$ qubit-wise-commuting bases that $(m_0,m_1,m_2)$ require ($1.8\times10^7$ shots per repetition). Over $8$ repetitions the mean estimates $(\widehat m_0,\widehat m_1,\widehat m_2)=(2.730,10.55,47.01)$ lie $0.87$, $0.72$ and $0.66$ standard errors (spread over repetitions divided by $\sqrt8$) from the exact $(2.737,10.61,47.64)$; for the $L=4$ chain ($8$ qubits, $126$ bases) every moment is recovered within $0.06$ standard errors. The number of bases is set by the Pauli support of $O_k$, not by the moment values, so it can be counted without the statevector once the probe shift $\langle\rho_\pi\rangle$ is known. That shift vanishes by the reflection symmetry of the open chain: for even $L$, reflection maps $\rho_\pi$ to $-\rho_\pi$, so $\langle\rho_\pi\rangle=0$ in a reflection-symmetric ground state. The ground states are not half filled ($2$, $4$ and $5$ electrons at $L=4$, $6$ and $8$, where $\langle\rho_\pi\rangle=0$ was checked numerically; it is assumed at $L=10$ and $12$). The committed script \texttt{src/\allowbreak estimator\_scaling.py} counts the bases this way and reproduces the count from the full eigendecomposition where affordable ($L\le8$). The full $(m_0,m_1,m_2)$ battery needs $126,357,721,1191,1837$ bases at $L=4,6,8,10,12$ ($8$ to $24$ qubits); the $(m_0,m_1)$ pair alone needs $19,35,51,67,83$, growing roughly linearly. The number of settings thus grows polynomially in this range, not exponentially. We do not turn this into a deployability claim: the counts-based recovery was validated only up to $12$ qubits and the basis count against full diagonalization only up to $L=8$; the shot budget and accuracy at $24$ qubits remain to be characterized; and a device would have to prepare the ground state that the classical check takes as given.

The estimator, including its off-diagonal content, is therefore measurable from a fixed shot budget in principle; it has not been run on a device here, and without mitigation the forecast bias at the reference depth is a large fraction of the signal. A job script that builds the rotated-basis circuits and applies this estimator (\texttt{src/\allowbreak hardware\_blind\_job.py}) is in the repository; its state preparation is a placeholder, it has not been run on a quantum processor, and an off-diagonal test on hardware requires a calibrated mitigation of this bias or lower gate error.

For the bond-basis estimator of Fig.~\ref{fig:circuit}, no off-diagonal moment has been measured on a device in this work. Under global depolarizing noise of strength $p$ the estimate of $m_1=\langle 0|O_1|0\rangle$, with $O_1=J^\dagger(H-E_0)J$, becomes $(1-p)\,m_1+p\operatorname{Tr}(O_1)/\dim\mathcal H$, a bias of $p\,[\operatorname{Tr}(O_1)/\dim\mathcal H-m_1]$. For the $L=8$ device Hamiltonian and $J=c^{\dagger}_{0\uparrow}$, $\operatorname{Tr}(O_1)/\dim\mathcal H=6.80$ against $m_1=2.10$ (ratio $3.24$), so the bias is upward. The forecast uses as a surrogate a bond-basis probe with nearly the same ratio, the $q=\pi$ density probe of the open $L=6$ chain at $U/t=4$ ($m_1=10.6$, $\operatorname{Tr}(O_1)/\dim\mathcal H=34.3$, ratio $3.23$), with no error mitigation modeled. An exact density-matrix simulation with local two-qubit depolarizing noise at $\varepsilon=2.5\times10^{-3}$ puts its first-moment bias at $13\%$ of the signal at $100$ controlled-$Z$ (CZ) gates and $38\%$ at $295$ CZ; global depolarizing noise at the same depths gives $49\%$ and $116\%$, and a coherent-$ZZ$ model $47\%$ at $295$ CZ. The local value is exact for a static noise model that omits the operator spreading of a real circuit, leakage and readout error, so it is expected to underestimate, but is not proven to bound, the bias of a real circuit. The depth of $295$ CZ is the median transpiled CZ count of our $L=8$ sampler circuits on \texttt{ibm\_fez} (range $278$--$313$; the $12$-qubit circuits of the companion's family need $213$, as deployed in our 2026-08-21 jobs and in a transpiler reproduction) and serves as a reference: the bond-basis estimator needs a ground-state preparation, whose depth is not established here. The global-depolarizing part of this bias is affine in $p$ with a classically computable offset $\operatorname{Tr}(O_1)/\dim\mathcal H$, so noise renormalization removes it once $p$ is calibrated~\cite{Vovrosh2021,Kiss2025}; the local, coherent and leakage parts are not removed this way. Related quantities have been estimated on IBM hardware: Hamiltonian moments in circuits of up to about $500$ CNOT gates~\cite{Vallury2023} and, with error mitigation, the Liouvillian recursion behind the Green's functions of a four-site Hubbard model~\cite{Leblanc2026}. The particle-number post-selection that protects the diagonal channel does not survive the bond-basis rotations. A device-side off-diagonal test therefore requires a calibrated mitigation of this bias or lower gate error; no device datum here bears on off-diagonal content (Table~\ref{tab:scope}).

The off-diagonal limitation concerns the bond-basis estimator of $m_1$, not every discriminating on-device channel. A diagonal one exists: the momentum distribution $n_k$, the occupied spectral weight at momentum $k$, with the sum rules
\[
n_k=\int_{\omega<0}A(k,\omega)\,d\omega=\langle c^\dagger_{k\sigma}c_{k\sigma}\rangle,\qquad
\int A(k,\omega)\,d\omega=\langle\{c_{k\sigma},c^\dagger_{k\sigma}\}\rangle=1 ,
\]
with $\omega$ measured from a chemical potential in the gap.
The total weight is pinned at every $k$ and is blind to how the weight splits between the occupied and empty sides; $n_k$ is not.

\emph{The $n_k$ channel.} For the momentum distribution $n_k$ above: in the doped $L=6$ ground state ($N_\uparrow=N_\downarrow=2$: density $2/3$ per site, i.e.\ $1/3$ per spin orbital, the value a fully flattened $n_k$ takes; committed script \texttt{src/\allowbreak nk\_falsifier.py}) $n_k$ has a finite-size step ${\max_k n_k-\min_k n_k}=0.91$ at $U/t=4$ and $0.80$ at $U/t=8$; in the thermodynamic limit the one-dimensional step softens to a Luttinger power-law cusp. A reconstruction that flattens the dispersion moves $\bar n_k$ toward this filling value, while the true $n_k$ keeps the step. (At half filling the chain is a Mott insulator and $n_k$ is analytic, so this discriminant targets doped sectors, not the half-filled spectrum of Fig.~\ref{fig:akw}.) The occupation $n_k$ is a number operator in the momentum basis, read out diagonally after a number-conserving fermionic Fourier transform (a Givens network of $L(L-1)/2$ rotations per spin sector). The particle-number and spin post-selection that protects the coverage channel therefore survives the basis change. The loss of post-selection and the full-space trace-to-value bias that affect the bond-basis rotations do not arise; depolarization acts within the sector instead, as described next. In an in-sector depolarizing model with post-selection at $\varepsilon=2.5\times10^{-3}$, the ${\sim}60$-CZ transform adds a flattening bias of ${\approx}9\%$ of the step; this assumes all-to-all connectivity, before the fermionic-\textsc{swap} routing a real device needs, which adds gates, so we expect it to underestimate the bias on hardware. The device estimate $\widehat n_k$ is not immune to noise. Depolarization flattens it toward the same filling value that a dispersion corruption does, so it discriminates only corruptions whose flattening exceeds the device's own, and it inherits the depolarization of the state preparation. Readout error and coherent or leakage noise, which need not flatten toward filling, are not modeled, and post-selection costs shots.

\emph{The sealed $n_k$ test.} A pre-registered on-device $n_k$ test (doped $L=6$ ring, $U/t=8$, $12$ qubits) was sealed on 2026-09-02 (manifest SHA-256 \texttt{4962\ldots ae80}). Its day-of rule runs the test only on a $12$-qubit chain whose every qubit has calibrated $T_1\ge150\,\mu$s and $T_2\ge100\,\mu$s, with no chain CZ error above twice the device median, and otherwise forfeits the window. Launched twice on 2026-09-03 against \texttt{ibm\_fez}, it submitted nothing: the first launch stopped in chain selection on a qubit without a calibrated $T_2$, and the second forfeited (longest compliant chain $7$ qubits; the $T_1$ and $T_2$ floors bind). Calibrations of \texttt{ibm\_fez}, \texttt{ibm\_marrakesh} and \texttt{ibm\_kingston} re-queried through 2026-09-05 forfeit under the sealed rule as well, and a post-seal amendment with looser floors failed its pre-committed simulated gate and was not adopted. No $n_k$ job was submitted (\texttt{data/\allowbreak 2026-09-05\_nk\_device\_\allowbreak attempt\_record.json}, which also records that no job on the account was created after the seal; history in \texttt{docs/\allowbreak PROCESS\_RECORD.md}).

\section{A code-scalability check beyond exact diagonalization}
\label{app:L24}
\label{sec:L24}
On an open Hubbard chain at $L=24$, half filling and $U/t=8$ ($S_z=0$ sector of dimension ${\approx}7.31\times10^{12}$), with the total current $J$ as probe, $\mathcal S$ is taken from Born-rule samples of a TeNPy DMRG ground state~\cite{TeNPy2024}. The subspace ground state $|0_\mathcal S\rangle$ of $H_\mathcal S=\Pi_\mathcal S H\Pi_\mathcal S$ on the sampled configurations is found by sparse diagonalization, and both branches are evaluated on it. From $1000$ to $15\,000$ shots $\Delta_0$ falls from $40.6$ to $24.5$ (DMRG $m_0=55.0$); at $500$ shots the subspace ground level is two-fold degenerate, and $\Delta_0$ ranges over $40.1$--$44.5$ within it. The relative error of the reconstruction's total weight $\bar m_0$ against DMRG falls from $0.89$ to $0.55$ ($0.95$ at $500$ shots); since $\widehat m_0$ varies little, both $\Delta_0$ and this error are dominated by the falling $\bar m_0$ and move together by construction. The estimate $\widehat m_0=46.5$--$49.5$ itself lies $10$--$15.5\%$ below the DMRG $m_0$: the residual measures the leak, not the distance to the truth. Even at $15\,000$ shots the subspace energy $e_0=-2.90\,t$ is far above the DMRG $E_0=-7.66\,t$, so a large residual is expected. There $|\mathcal S|=13\,235$ and $\varphi_\mathcal S$ lives on $4.4\times10^5$ configurations; both branches are evaluated at $k=0$ and $1$ ($\Delta_1=121$ against $\widehat m_1=393$), and $\widehat m_2$, which needs $(H-e_0)\varphi_\mathcal S$ on the configurations that $H$ connects to that set, was not evaluated beyond exact diagonalization. The check shows that the code runs at this scale; it is not a measure of power and not a beyond-classical claim.
